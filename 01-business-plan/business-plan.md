## Executive Summary

**The problem.** Owners of apartment buildings, hotels and offices compete on amenities, but pools and gym expansions cost millions, take years, and need space the building does not have. Roofs sit empty. Meanwhile sauna and contrast therapy have become the wellness amenity residents, guests and employees ask for by name.

## The Opportunity

Large buildings are in an amenity arms race, and the roof is the one place most of them can still add a signature amenity in weeks rather than years.

**Amenities decide leases and bookings.** Class A apartment buildings compete on their amenity package, hotels compete on wellness, and office landlords compete on reasons to come in. The amenities that used to win (a gym, a lounge, a coworking floor) are now table stakes.

**The expensive options are gone.** A rooftop pool costs millions, needs structural reinforcement and takes years of permitting. Spa floors need plumbing, mechanical and space the building does not have. Most owners have no amenity left to add that residents and guests will actually talk about.

**The roof is empty.** Most large flat-roofed buildings carry a mechanical zone and nothing else, or a deck that is used three months a year. It is the most underused square footage in the building, and it has the best view.

**Sauna has gone mainstream.** Sauna social clubs and bathhouses are opening across North American cities, cold plunge and contrast therapy have crossed into everyday wellness, and hotel guests now rate a sauna alongside a pool. Sourced figures for each of these trends are in the Market Analysis section.

**Nobody sells it as a product.** An owner who wants a rooftop sauna today commissions a custom build: an architect, a structural engineer, a sauna builder, an electrician, a crane company, several permits and a year of coordination. Vantage packages all of that into one contract, one from-price and a six-to-ten-week timeline.

## How It Works

From signed contract to first session takes 6–10 weeks for Perch and Lodge and 10–14 weeks for Summit, in six steps that Vantage runs end to end. The owner signs once and attends two meetings.

1. **Site assessment (week 0).** A free 48-hour desktop check of the roof plans, electrical capacity and access route, followed by a site visit. Output: a model recommendation and a firm installed price.
2. **Structural review and engineering package (weeks 1–2).** A licensed structural engineer reviews the roof's as-built drawings and signs off the load-spreading base layout, ballast and parapet tie-backs. Our electrical partner designs the three-phase feed.
3. **Design and permit set (weeks 2–4).** Stamped drawings, building and electrical permits, fire marshal sign-off where the jurisdiction requires it, and crane or street-closure permits for module deliveries.
4. **Factory build (weeks 2–7, in parallel).** Panel fabrication, module assembly, heater and controls fit-out, glazing, and a factory quality check that includes a four-hour heat and ventilation test before anything leaves the floor.
5. **Install (weeks 7–9).** One crane day for a module, or a freight-elevator carry and on-roof assembly for a flat-pack. Set the base, place the suite, connect power and drain. One to six days on site depending on model.
6. **Commissioning, training and launch (weeks 8–10).** Heat tests, Vantage Access set up for residents or guests, staff training, and a launch event. Vantage Care starts the same day.

**What the building provides.** A clear roof area (about 5 × 6 m for Perch, 6 × 8 m for Lodge, 8 × 12 m for Summit including the terrace), a three-phase electrical feed or a quoted upgrade, a roof drain within reach, and an access route (crane position or freight elevator). Site preparation outside the suite itself is quoted separately, typically $8,000–$25,000.

## Business Model and Pricing

Vantage earns a 40% gross margin on every installed suite, then about $8,000 a year per building at a 63% margin from service and software for as long as the sauna is on the roof. By the end of 2031 the installed base of 627 buildings represents roughly $5.0M of annual recurring revenue.

**Three ways to buy.**

- **Purchase** (primary). 50% deposit at contract, 40% at delivery, 10% at commissioning. Deposits cover the factory build, so each project is cash-positive for Vantage from day one.

## Go-to-Market Strategy

We sell to the owner or asset manager who controls many roofs, prove the product on a demo rooftop and three pilot buildings in New York City in 2027, and then expand one metro at a time with a local install crew, engineer of record and crane partner in each.

**Segments, in priority order.**

1. **Class A and B+ multifamily.** The buyer is the owner or asset manager; the motive is retention, lease-up speed and a rent premium on a building that has run out of amenities to add. Purchase or Vantage Flex.
2. **Lifestyle and upscale hotels.** The buyer is the owner or brand wellness team; the motive is a paid guest experience and a rooftop story for marketing. Purchase, Flex or the capped revenue-share pilot.
3. **Class A offices.** The buyer is the landlord; the motive is a reason to come in and a tenant-retention tool. Purchase or Flex.
4. **Everything else with a roof and a membership:** senior living, student housing, co-living and private clubs.

**Channels.** Direct enterprise sales to owners and asset managers; property-management companies as referral partners; architects and developers specifying Vantage into new builds; hotel brand wellness teams; and the industry calendar (NMHC, NAA Apartmentalize, ULI, hotel investment conferences).

**Sales process.** An inbound site-assessment request or an outbound approach to a target list, a 48-hour desktop check of the roof plans and electrical capacity, a site visit, a firm proposal, a structural review and a contract. We plan on a three-to-six-month cycle and on 1.4 buildings per customer in 2027 rising to 2.5 by 2031 as portfolio roll-outs follow first installs.

**Pilot programme.** A demo rooftop in New York City acts as showroom and test bed. Three lighthouse buildings (one apartment building, one hotel, one office) are installed at cost in 2027 in exchange for case studies, usage data and references. Together these cost about $500,000 of the seed round.

**Metro expansion.** New York City in 2027; Chicago, Boston and Toronto in 2028; Denver, Seattle and Minneapolis in 2029. Each metro opens with a local install crew, a structural engineer of record, an electrical partner and a crane partner, and is fed by the demo rooftop and references from the previous metro.

## Operations and Manufacturing

Vantage builds in a leased assembly facility near New York City, buys panels and heaters from established suppliers, and installs with its own crews supported by local crane and electrical partners in each metro.

**Facility.** A 15,000 sq ft light-industrial unit within 90 minutes of Manhattan, fitted in H1 2027 for about $450,000: two assembly bays, a glazing station, a controls bench and a heat-test cell. One bay builds about three Lodge-equivalent suites a week on a single shift; a second line in 2029 doubles that. At 300 installs a year (2031) we run two lines on two shifts or add a contract assembler in the Midwest.

**Supply chain.** Wall and roof panels are made to our drawings by a structural-insulated-panel fabricator; thermally modified cladding and thermo-aspen interiors come from Nordic and North American mills; heaters and controllers from a commercial heater maker with a UL 875 listing; glazing from a regional tempered-glass supplier; steel base frames from a local fabricator. Every component has a second source before we exceed 100 installs a year.

**Build quality.** Every suite completes a factory checklist and a four-hour heat and ventilation test before shipping. Moisture, temperature and heater-cycle data from the test are stored against the unit's serial number and feed the Care plan.

**Install logistics.** Perch and Lodge ship by default as flat-pack kits of panels no larger than 2.3 × 1.2 m and no piece over 120 kg, carried up the freight elevator and assembled on the roof in one to three days, which avoids crane permits and street closures in dense cities; where a crane is practical they ship as a single module and are set in one lift. Summit ships as two modules plus the deck and plunge and is always craned. Each metro has an install crew of three, a structural engineer of record, an electrical partner and a rigging partner. One crew completes 40–50 installs a year.

**Capacity plan.** 12 installs in 2027 with one crew; 40 in 2028 with two crews in two metros; 95 in 2029 with four crews; 180 in 2030 with eight; 300 in 2031 with twelve crews across the launch metros.

**After install.** Vantage Care technicians (one per 60 installed buildings) run quarterly inspections, replace heater stones annually, refinish benches every two to three years and respond to faults within 24 hours. Vantage Access gives the building manager usage, booking and fault data; the same data tells us when a heater element or sensor is due before it fails.

## Engineering, Safety and Compliance

Every Vantage install is reviewed by a licensed structural engineer, uses a UL 875 listed electric heater in a non-combustible envelope, and is permitted with the local building department and fire marshal before it goes on the roof.

**Structure.** The galvanized steel base frame carries the module's weight (about 2,300 kg for Perch, 4,500 kg for Lodge and 8,000 kg for Summit before ballast) through adjustable feet and pads to the roof's beams and columns rather than spreading it across the deck. Suites are designed for occupiable roofs rated 100 psf (4.8 kPa), the assembly-use load in NYC Building Code Table 1607.1; Summit's filled cold plunge is placed over primary structure, and roofs rated lower (many New York residential decks are 60 psf) are reinforced as site preparation. The engineer of record confirms every placement, and we never penetrate the membrane: the frame is held by ballast trays and parapet tie-back brackets designed to the site's wind speed under ASCE 7. Snow, drift and occupant loads are included in every review.

**Fire.** All-electric heating with no combustion on the roof. Walls use non-combustible mineral-wool insulation and fibre-cement sheathing behind the cladding; the roof is standing-seam steel. Heater clearances follow the listing, the heater guard is standard, and the interior is capped at 90 °C (194 °F), the limit of listed commercial heaters. Where the jurisdiction asks, we add a heat detector tied to the building's fire alarm and sprinkler coverage at the roof access.

**Electrical.** Heaters are ETL/UL 875 listed, run on 208–240 V three-phase, and are wired by a licensed electrician on a dedicated circuit with a local disconnect to NEC Articles 422 and 424 and the heater's listing instructions (the NEC has no sauna-specific article). The controller enforces a 60-minute session timer, an over-temperature cut-off and a door-open sensor, and schedules operating hours.

**People safety.** Smart-lock access only to booked users; an occupancy sensor; an emergency stop that cuts the heater and alerts the building; exterior signage on temperature, hydration and session length; a drinking-water and first-aid station; non-slip flooring, a 350 mm bench step and guarded heater. The interior is capped at 90 °C (194 °F). Session limits and age rules are set by the building in Vantage Access.

**Accessibility.** Every suite has a level or ramped entry, an 813 mm (32 in) clear outward-swinging door, a 1,525 mm (60 in) turning circle in the vestibule or on the entry porch, an ADA transfer bench 450–480 mm (17–19 in) high and 1,070 mm (42 in) long at the lower tier, and controls within reach range, so a building can meet ADA requirements for an amenity on an accessible roof route.

**Permits.** Building permit, electrical permit, fire marshal review where required, and crane or street-closure permits for module lifts. We keep a permit template per metro so the package is the same each time and the review is fast.

**Insurance and liability.** Vantage carries product and completed-operations liability insurance and names the building owner as additional insured for the install. The building's own liability cover extends to the amenity in the same way as a gym or pool; Vantage Care includes a documented inspection record that insurers ask for.

**Certification path.** UL 875 Edition 10 (2024) explicitly covers "a heater unit combined with a prefabricated rigid room", so the seed round funds ETL/UL 875 listing of the whole Vantage module (heater, controls and room) rather than the heater alone, third-party testing of the wall panel for non-combustibility, and the engineer-stamped base kit for occupiable roofs rated 100 psf, so that structural review becomes a site check rather than a design project.

## Risks and Mitigations

The risks that could stop Vantage are structural and regulatory first, safety second and sales third; each has a mitigation built into the plan and the budget.

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roofs that cannot take the load | A large share of older buildings have roofs rated for maintenance only, which cuts the addressable base and lengthens sales | Free 48-hour desktop check before any site visit; the base frame carries the load to beams and columns; target occupiable roofs rated 100 psf and newer Class A stock first |
| Permit and fire-code delays | A slow or hostile authority adds months and cost per install | Permit template per metro, UL/ETL listing, non-combustible envelope, early fire-marshal meetings, a code consultant on the advisory board |
| A safety incident | One burn, slip or heat-related injury would damage the brand and raise insurance costs | Session timers, temperature cap, emergency stop, occupancy sensor, signage, access only to booked users, inspection records in Vantage Care, product liability cover |
| Longer sales cycles than planned | Capital decisions by owners can take two budget cycles, which pushes the 2028 install target and the Series A | Vantage Flex removes the capital objection; pilot references; portfolio roll-outs; a 25% lower install case is modelled and still funded through 2028 |
| Supply and cost inflation | Timber, steel, heaters and freight are volatile and could compress the 40% margin | Two sources per component, 90-day price locks with suppliers, from-prices reviewed quarterly, add-ons priced separately |
| A prefab sauna brand enters rooftops | A maker with factory scale could undercut on unit price | Lead on engineering kit, code package, software and references; be the installed base they would rather acquire than fight |
| Weather and installation risk | Crane days are weather-dependent and an install error on a roof is expensive | Flat-pack elevator option, weather windows in the schedule, factory heat test, install checklist and sign-off by the engineer of record |
| Key-person and hiring risk | The plan depends on three senior hires in 2027 | Recruiting starts before the round closes; advisors cover engineering and code in the interim; founder equity pool reserved for the three roles |

### C. Frequently asked questions from building owners

**Will my roof take the weight?** Our suites are designed for occupiable roofs rated 100 psf, the code load for roofs people gather on, and the steel base carries the weight to the building's beams and columns rather than spreading it across the deck. A licensed structural engineer reviews the point loads on every site before we quote; if your deck is rated lower (many New York residential decks are 60 psf) we quote the reinforcement as site preparation.

**Do you penetrate the roof?** No. The frame sits on load-spreading pads and is held by ballast and parapet tie-backs. Your roof warranty is unaffected.

**What about fire code and insurance?** The heater is electric and UL 875 listed, the walls use non-combustible insulation and sheathing, the roof is steel, and the interior is capped at 90 °C (194 °F). We carry product and completed-operations liability and name you as additional insured; your own liability cover treats the sauna like a gym or pool.

**How long does it take?** Six to ten weeks from contract to first session for Perch and Lodge, ten to fourteen for Summit. On-site work is one to six days.

**Who maintains it?** Vantage Care: quarterly inspections, annual heater stones, bench refinishing, parts and 24-hour support, from $350 a month.

**How do residents or guests book?** Through the Vantage Access app. The smart lock opens only for a booked session, the timer runs 60 minutes, and the building sets hours, age rules and, for hotels, prices.

**Can a hotel charge for it?** Yes. Hotels typically price sessions at $35–$60 per guest, sell memberships to locals, and bill through their own systems or through Vantage Access.

**What if we have no three-phase power on the roof?** We design the feed with our electrical partner and quote the upgrade as site preparation; most large buildings have three-phase service and only need a new circuit to the roof.

### D. Glossary

- **ACV:** average contract value, the blended hardware price of an installed suite including add-ons ($140,000).
- **Flat-pack:** the suite delivered as panels sized for a freight elevator and assembled on the roof.
- **Load-spreading base:** the steel frame, pads and sleepers that distribute the suite's weight across the roof structure.
- **psf / kPa:** pounds per square foot / kilopascals, the units for roof load ratings.
- **UL 875:** the US safety standard for electric sauna heaters.
- **NEC Articles 422 and 424:** the US electrical code articles (422 for appliances and 424 for fixed electric heating equipment) that govern the heater wiring.
- **Vantage Access:** the booking, access-control and analytics software, $249 a month per building.
- **Vantage Care:** the service plan, $350–$900 a month by model.
- **Vantage Flex:** the 84-month lease offered through a finance partner.
