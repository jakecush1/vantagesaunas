Vantage Sauna Co. — Business Plan (Canada)

Nov 1, 2026 · @jake

Executive Summary

Vantage Sauna Co. turns the most underused space in a building, its roof, into its most-loved amenity: a factory-built, all-electric rooftop sauna suite, installed in days and run through our own booking software and service plan. We launch in Vancouver and Victoria in 2027 and add Toronto in 2028. To get there we are raising about C$2.15M of equity (C$150,000 of pre-seed now and a C$2.0M seed round by the end of March 2027) plus a C$350,000 government-backed equipment loan. That pays for Canadian engineering and certification, a demo Lodge, a Fraser Valley workshop and the first 19 installed buildings by the end of 2028.

The problem. Rental towers, hotels and condos compete on amenities, but pools and spa floors cost millions, take years and need space the building does not have. Roofs sit empty. New rental towers are struggling to fill: 59% of the GTHA's 2025 rental completions were still unleased at year end. Meanwhile Vancouver alone has about 20 sauna studios charging C$24–55 a visit.

The product. Three models, all prefabricated in the Fraser Valley and all electric:

  • Perch, 4–6 seats, from C$99,000 installed.
  • Lodge, 8–10 seats with a changing vestibule, indoor shower and weatherproof lockers, from C$179,000 installed.
  • Summit, 12–14 seats with a changing room, cold plunge, outdoor shower and terrace, from C$329,000 installed.

In every hot room both bench tiers run wall to wall along the back wall and face a full-length, privacy-tinted glass front, so every seat has a view out. Heaters carry a Canadian mark (cETLus, cULus or CSA) and run on 208 V. In BC the steel base is bolted to the structure with engineered anchors, flashed by a roofer, to meet seismic rules. Prices exclude GST, PST and HST.

The business model. Buildings buy outright (50% deposit, 40% at delivery, 10% at commissioning) or lease through Vantage Flex from C$2,150 a month. Every building then pays for Vantage Care (service, C$450–1,150 a month) and Vantage Access (booking, smart-lock access and analytics, C$300 a month). Hardware earns about 42% gross margin at steady state. Recurring revenue of about C$10,048 per building a year earns 63%.

The market. The three cities hold about 4,770 hotels, large condo projects and rental towers. We estimate about 1,140 are suitable today: about C$207M of hardware at our C$181,500 average contract, plus about C$11.5M a year of recurring revenue. The base plan needs 122 of them, about 11%, by 2031.

The plan. 5 installs in 2027, 14 in 2028, 24 in 2029, 34 in 2030 and 45 in 2031. Revenue grows from C$0.78M to C$9.16M, EBITDA turns positive in 2029 and reaches C$1.33M in 2031, and cash never falls below C$0.84M. The base case needs no Series A.

The team. Founded by Jacob [Last name], Founder and CEO. The first hires in 2027 are a head of engineering and product (P.Eng.), a sales and partnerships lead for hotels and rental owners, and an operations and project manager.

The Opportunity

Canadian rental towers, hotels and condos need an amenity that sets them apart, and the roof is the one place most of them can still add one in weeks rather than years.

New rental towers are fighting for tenants. In the GTHA, two-thirds of rental buildings completed since 2000 offered incentives in Q4 2025, and 35% offered two months free (Urbanation, Jan 2026). In Metro Vancouver, buildings with 200+ units ran 11.5% vacancy in October 2025, and stock built since mid-2022 ran 14.8% (CMHC Rental Market Survey 2025). An owner in that position needs a reason for tenants to choose the building.

Hotels cannot add rooms, so they need more revenue per guest. Vancouver's hotel stock has not grown since 2002 (Hotel Management Network, Jun 2025). Its hotels ran 78.4% occupancy at a C$284 average daily rate in 2025, the highest of any major Canadian market (CoStar via Hospitality Net). Fairmont Pacific Rim already sells C$40 Nordic sessions on its sixth-floor pool deck (Fairmont).

The expensive options are gone. A rooftop pool or a spa floor needs structural work, plumbing, mechanical space and years of permits. Most owners have no amenity left to add that residents and guests will talk about.

The roof is empty. Most large flat-roofed buildings carry a mechanical zone and nothing else. It is the most underused space in the building, and it has the best view.

Sauna has gone mainstream in Canada. Metro Vancouver has about 20 circuit-sauna venues (The Peak, Aug 2026). Toronto's Othership charges C$58 a class (Narcity, Mar 2026), and Victoria has HAVN's floating sauna in the Inner Harbour (Tourism Victoria). Regular users already pay C$130–280 a month for memberships (Scout Magazine, Oct 2025).

Nobody sells it as a product. An owner who wants a rooftop sauna today hires an architect, a structural engineer, a sauna builder, an electrician, a roofer and a crane company. The only commercial rooftop sauna we found in Canada is at the Hampton Inn & Suites in downtown Vancouver, known from travel listings (Destination Vancouver). Vantage packages the whole job into one contract and one from-price.

Construction system (shared by all models).

  • Base: hot-dip galvanized steel frame on adjustable feet and rubber load-spreading pads that carry the module to the roof's beams and columns. In Vancouver and Victoria (seismic category SC4) friction may not resist earthquake forces, so the frame is bolted to the structure with 4–8 engineered anchors, flashed by a roofer, and ballast is kept to a minimum. In Toronto a ballasted base with no roof penetrations can still pass where the site is SC1 or SC2 and the wind checks pass. Designed for occupied roofs rated 4.8 kPa (NBC, BCBC and OBC Table 4.1.5.3).
  • Walls, outside to in: 20 mm thermally modified ash or pine cladding, 25 mm ventilated cavity, breathable membrane, 12 mm fibre-cement sheathing, 90 mm non-combustible mineral wool, aluminium-foil vapour barrier rated to 120 °C, 22 mm batten gap, 15 mm thermo-aspen interior panelling. About 185 mm in total. Towers of noncombustible construction get a noncombustible wood-look cladding option (or a CAN/ULC-S134 test) and fire-retardant or tested ceiling boards with a flame-spread rating of 25 or less.
  • Roof: standing-seam steel on a 3° mono-pitch with an integrated gutter to the roof drain. The module roof is designed for 2.0 kPa of snow plus drift (the code roof snow load is 1.64 kPa in Vancouver and 1.12 kPa in Toronto).
  • Glazing: the whole outside wall of the hot room is a double-glazed tempered unit with a privacy-tinted reflective bronze outer pane and a low-iron inner pane, in a thermally broken aluminium frame. By day bathers see out and the roof sees a dark pane. At night the effect reverses, so the night lighting scene keeps the hot room dim; an interior blind is optional.
  • Heater: commercial electric, Canadian-marked (cETLus, cULus or CSA) to CSA C22.2 No. 164-18, rated for 208 V three-phase. The controller has schedules, an over-temperature cut-off, a door sensor and a hard-wired 1-hour timer with no override, as Ontario's Rule 62-222 requires. Buildings with only 600 V service get the transformer kit.
  • Interior: thermo-aspen benches in two tiers along the back wall, side wall to side wall, facing the glass. The upper bench is 1,150 mm from the floor and 700 mm deep, with the ceiling 1,150–1,250 mm above it. The lower bench is 700 mm high and 500 mm deep, with a 350 mm step by the door. The far end of the lower tier is a transfer section 465 mm high and 1,070 mm long, kept as best practice. The door is an 850 mm clear (900 mm leaf) outward-swinging glass door in the near side wall, and the Lodge and Summit hot rooms have a 1,500 mm turning space (CSA B651). Non-slip composite floor over aluminium grating with a drain; mechanical ventilation at 9–12 L/s per person; dimmable 2700 K LED scenes.
  • Fire and safety: sprinkler-ready module with a high-temperature head, a horn and strobe tied to the building's fire alarm, a Bluetooth/NFC smart lock tied to Vantage Access, an occupancy sensor, an emergency stop, a 90 °C interior cap, safety signage and a first-aid and drinking-water station.

Delivery. Perch and Lodge ship by default as flat-pack panel kits that fit a standard elevator: no panel larger than 2.0 × 1.1 m and no piece over 120 kg. They are assembled on the roof, which avoids crane permits and street closures downtown. Summit ships as a craneable module, and Perch or Lodge can too where a staging street exists. Kits for Toronto travel by truck from the Fraser Valley. On-site time is 1–2 days (Perch), 2–3 days (Lodge) and 4–6 days (Summit). Contract to first session takes 12–20 weeks for Perch and Lodge and 16–26 weeks for Summit, including permits, owner votes and any pool permit.

Software and service. Vantage Access (C$300 a month per building) handles booking, smart-lock access, session timers within the 1-hour hard cut-off, usage analytics and, for hotels, paid-session pricing. It runs on a white-label platform at launch. Vantage Care (C$450, C$700 or C$1,150 a month by model) covers quarterly inspections, heater stones, bench refinishing, parts, 24-hour support and water-quality logging for any plunge.

How It Works

From signed contract to first session takes 12–20 weeks for Perch and Lodge and 16–26 weeks for Summit, in seven steps that Vantage runs end to end. Most of that time is owner votes and permits; the factory build runs in parallel.

  1. Site assessment (week 0). A desktop check of the roof plans, the electrical service (120/208 V or 347/600 V), the access route and the site's seismic category, then a site visit. Output: a model recommendation and a firm installed price, with site preparation quoted separately.
  2. Owner approval (weeks 1–8). A rental or hotel owner signs directly. A BC strata needs a 3/4 vote of the votes cast at a general meeting, and a further 3/4 vote to fund it from a special levy or the contingency reserve fund. An Ontario condo board can proceed with notice to owners when the cost is under 10% of the annual budget; above that, owners of 66⅔% of all units must vote yes. We supply the information-session kit and sample resolutions.
  3. Engineering and drawings (weeks 2–4). A structural engineer licensed in BC (EGBC) or Ontario (PEO) designs the anchors or ballast and checks the 4.8 kPa occupied-roof load, snow drift, wind (NBC 4.1.7) and seismic forces. Our electrical engineer designs the 208 V feed or the transformer kit.
  4. Permits (weeks 4–12). A development permit where Vancouver needs one, the building permit, the electrical permit (City of Vancouver, City of Victoria, or an ESA notification in Ontario), plumbing, a health-authority permit for any cold plunge, and a street-use permit for a crane lift. Toronto must decide a complete application within 20 business days (30 for high buildings); Vancouver's median for commercial renovation permits was 32 days in mid-2024.
  5. Factory build (4–6 weeks, in parallel). Panel fabrication, heater and controls fit-out, glazing, and a heat and ventilation test in the Fraser Valley workshop before anything ships.
  6. Install (1–6 days on site). An elevator carry and on-roof assembly for a flat-pack, or one crane lift for a module. In BC a roofer flashes the anchors. Set the base, place the suite, connect power and drain.
  7. Commissioning, training and launch. Electrical inspection, heat tests, Vantage Access set up for residents or guests, staff training and a launch event. Vantage Care starts the same day.

What the building provides. A clear roof area (about 5 × 6 m for Perch, 6 × 8 m for Lodge and 8 × 12 m for Summit including the terrace). A barrier-free route to the roof, which Ontario requires for rooftop amenity spaces. A roof exit and guards at least 1,070 mm high. An electrical service with capacity, or a quoted upgrade. A roof drain within reach, and an elevator or crane position for delivery.

Who decides, and how fast. A rental tower or hotel has one owner who decides, so the sales cycle is 2–4 months. Toronto's 710 private rental towers were mostly built in 1960–84 and belong to REITs, pension funds and private landlords that buy for whole portfolios. Condos take 4–9 months. In BC a significant change to common property needs a 3/4 vote of the votes cast; a special levy needs 3/4; reserve-fund spending needs 3/4, or a majority if the depreciation report recommends it (Strata Property Act s. 71). In Ontario, owners of 66⅔% of all units must vote yes if the cost exceeds 10% of the annual budget, the board can proceed with notice below that, and the reserve fund cannot pay for new amenities (Condominium Act, 1998, s. 97). A Lodge, at C$202,270 with HST, stays under the 10% line in Toronto condos of roughly 300 units or more (est.).

Why buildings will buy.

  • Rental owners are paying to fill towers. GTHA asking rents averaged C$2,916 a month but C$2,565 after incentives (Urbanation, Jan 2026). In a 300-unit tower that gap costs about C$1.26M a year; a suite that closed a tenth of it would be worth about C$126,000 a year, so a Lodge would pay back in under two years (est.).
  • Hotels can earn from it. A 300-room downtown Vancouver hotel at 80% occupancy has about 430 guests a night. If 3% book a C$40 session, that is about C$190,000 a year; Vantage's 35% revenue share is about C$66,000, well above the C$24,000 annual minimum (est.).
  • Residents already pay for it. In a 300-unit tower where 10% of residents would otherwise pay a C$150 monthly membership, a building suite delivers about C$4,500 a month of value, more than the C$3,850 Lodge Flex lease (est.).

Why now.

  • The hotel pipeline is the largest in Canada: Toronto has 71 projects (11,665 rooms) and Vancouver 45 (8,562 rooms) (Lodging Econometrics Q3 2026).
  • Rental supply is at a 40-year high. The GTHA started 9,821 purpose-built rentals in 2025 with 27,815 under construction (Urbanation), and Vancouver approved 10,277 rental homes in 2025 (Daily Hive, Apr 2026). We brief the architects of these projects in 2027 for 2029–2031 deliveries.
  • Thermal bathing is scaling: Tevah opened a 60-person sauna in downtown Vancouver in December 2025 (BC Business, Nov 2025), and Therme plans 14 saunas at Ontario Place for 2029 (Spa Business, Jul 2025).

Launch markets. Vancouver and Victoria first, in 2027. They share one province, one strata law and one building code, and their hotels run full: Vancouver 78.4% occupancy at a C$284 average rate in 2025 (downtown 80.5% at C$342, Destination Canada), and Greater Victoria 79.9% from January to August 2025 (Destination Greater Victoria). Toronto follows in 2028 as the volume market, with about 60% of the suitable buildings; downtown hotels ran 75.3% at C$350 in 2025 (CBRE via Ontario Data Catalogue).

Open questions we will close at the pilots. No Canadian survey prices a sauna premium in rents, condo values or hotel rates. The hotel class split by city, the condo project counts (derived from CMHC unit counts) and the rental-tower counts for Vancouver and Victoria are our estimates. We will measure lease-up, rents and usage at the first buildings.

Business Model and Pricing

Vantage earns about 42% gross margin on each installed suite at steady state, then about C$10,048 a year per building at a 63% margin from service and software for as long as the sauna is on the roof. By the end of 2031 the 122 installed buildings bring in about C$1.2M of recurring revenue a year.

Three ways to buy.

  • Purchase (primary). 50% deposit at contract, 40% at delivery, 10% at commissioning. Deposits pay for materials before each build. Care and Access are billed monthly.
  • Vantage Flex. An 84-month lease through a Canadian equipment-finance partner, with Care and Access included. The partner pays Vantage in full at install, and the building books an operating cost instead of capital. Site preparation is billed separately.
  • Hotel revenue-share pilot. Up to three lighthouse hotels: no upfront cost, Vantage takes 35% of session revenue over a five-year term with a C$2,000 monthly minimum. Vancouver hotels already sell C$40 sessions (Fairmont Pacific Rim). The purpose is flagship references, not scale.

Why the recurring layer matters. It turns a one-off sale into a relationship with the owner, gives us usage data for the next sale in the portfolio, and makes the installed base an asset: a building on Vantage Access and Care calls us first when the next roof comes up.

Go-to-Market Strategy

We sell first to owners who decide alone (rental towers and hotels), prove the product with a demo Lodge and a lighthouse rooftop in Vancouver, and add Toronto in 2028 once the first BC buildings are live and referenceable.

Segments, in priority order.

  1. Purpose-built rental towers and business or serviced apartments. One decision-maker under lease-up pressure. The motive is faster lease-up, fewer concessions and tenant retention. Purchase or Vantage Flex. Furnished-apartment operators such as Corporate Stays and Blueground lease units inside these towers, so they are a channel and a demand signal rather than separate roofs.
  2. Upscale and lifestyle hotels. The owner or brand wellness team buys a paid guest experience and a rooftop story. Purchase, Flex or the revenue-share pilot (up to three hotels).
  3. Condos and strata. Slower, but the largest pool. The BC playbook is one general meeting with two 3/4 resolutions: approve the change, and fund it. The Ontario playbook keeps a Perch or Lodge under 10% of the annual budget in larger towers, so the board can proceed with notice. Both come with information-session kits and sample resolutions.
  4. Developers of new rental and hotel projects. We brief architects in 2027 for deliveries in 2029–2031.

Channels. Direct sales to owners and asset managers; property-management firms and strata managers as referral partners; architects and developers who specify Vantage into new projects; a demo Lodge in the Fraser Valley workshop; and a lighthouse rooftop in Vancouver.

Sales process. A site-assessment request or an outbound approach to a target list, a desktop check of the roof plans and electrical service, a site visit, a firm proposal, owner approval, and a contract with a 50% deposit. We plan on 2–4 months for rentals and hotels and 4–9 months for condos.

Pilot programme. Before the seed closes we aim for three signed letters of intent, at least one from a hotel and one from a rental owner. In 2027 we install five paid pilot suites at a 15% discount, in exchange for case studies, usage data and references. The goal is three lighthouse references by the end of 2027.

City expansion. Vancouver and Victoria in 2027, served from the Fraser Valley workshop. Toronto business development starts in Q2 2028, a Toronto operations lead joins in 2029 and a GTA staging bay opens in 2031. Flat-pack kits travel to Toronto by truck, at about C$7,500 of freight per install. Before the first Toronto sale we get an Ontario legal opinion on condo approvals and an ESA interpretation of the 1-hour timer rule.

Custom builds deliver a good sauna at a high and uncertain price. Local builders such as Vancouver Sauna and SaunaFin (Concord, Ontario) work inside existing spas and amenity floors, not on roofs. They are our competition for flagship hotel projects, and we will lose some of those on bespoke design.

Canadian sauna makers build good barrels and cabins for backyards and cottages. Nootka's cedar barrels sell for C$7,950–9,950 (Nootka Saunas), and Fritz Tiny Homes' commercial cabins start at C$77,850, delivered only (Fritz Tiny Homes). None offers rooftop engineering, seismic anchors, a Canadian-marked commercial heater package, installation or operating software. They are the most likely to follow us onto roofs, and the most likely acquirers. A cost-cutting strata council will compare us with a C$10,000 barrel. Our answer is commercial duty: a commercial heater, Canadian certification, an accessible bench, a smart lock and a service plan.

Social-sauna clubs are the hotels' real alternative. Othership charges C$58 a class in Toronto, Tevah runs a 60-person sauna in downtown Vancouver, and HAVN plans a Vancouver barge for mid-2027 after its Victoria harbour site (Daily Hive, Nov 2025). They prove the demand we sell against. A building with its own suite offers that experience without the trip or the membership.

Our defence is not a patent. It is the rooftop and seismic engineering kit, the Canadian code and permit package, the heater and module certification, the installed base on Vantage Access and Care, and references from the first buildings. Building them takes us most of 2027, and a follower would have to do the same.

Operations and Manufacturing

Vantage builds in a leased workshop in the Fraser Valley, buys heaters, glass and steel from established suppliers, and installs with its own crews and local roofing, electrical and crane partners.

Facility. About 4,000 sq ft of light-industrial space in Langley or Abbotsford at about C$22 per sq ft a year gross, leased from April 2027 (C$66,000 in 2027). The fit-out costs about C$180,000: 600 V three-phase power and a panel (C$35,000), an overhead hoist and racking (C$25,000), dust extraction (C$15,000), a panel saw, a used CNC router and finishing gear (C$70,000), an install kit with a stair-climber and lifting gear (C$20,000), and IT and a shop office (C$15,000). A used 16-ft box truck with a liftgate costs about C$55,000. We move to about 8,000 sq ft in Q2 2029 and add a GTA staging bay in 2031.

Supply chain. Steel base frames from a Canadian fabricator; wall and roof panels made in our workshop; thermally modified cladding and thermo-aspen interiors from Nordic and North American mills; tinted insulated glass from a regional supplier; and Canadian-marked commercial heaters, ideally of EU origin. Sauna heaters enter Canada duty-free from the EU, the UK, the US and Mexico. A 25% surtax on imported steel derivative goods (including prefabricated structures) and Canada's counter-tariffs on some US goods make Canadian steel and EU-made heaters the cheaper path. We collect the certificate number for every heater before we order, and every component gets a second source as volume grows.

Build quality. Every suite completes a factory checklist and a heat and ventilation test before it ships. Moisture, temperature and heater-cycle data from the test are stored against the unit's serial number and feed the Care plan. From 2029 we certify the workshop and panel kit to CSA A277. A certified module is deemed to comply with the Ontario Building Code, and in BC it spares inspectors from opening up concealed work on site.

Install logistics. Perch and Lodge ship as flat-pack kits of panels no larger than 2.0 × 1.1 m, with no piece over 120 kg. Crews carry them up the elevator, using a stair-climber where needed, and assemble them on the roof in one to three days. Summit ships as a module and needs a crane and a street-use permit, so we sell it to roofs with a staging street; a downtown lift can add C$15,000–35,000. In BC a roofing partner flashes the seismic anchors so the roof warranty stays intact. Toronto kits travel by truck from the Fraser Valley.

Capacity plan. The base plan rises from 5 installs in 2027 to 45 in 2031; the yearly figures by city are in the Financial Plan. Install labour sits in cost of goods, and we add a Toronto crew as Toronto installs start in 2028.

After install. Vantage Care visits each building every quarter: inspections, heater stones, bench refinishing, parts and 24-hour support, plus water-quality logging for any plunge. Vantage Access gives the building manager usage, booking and fault data, and the same data tells us when a heater element or sensor is due before it fails.

Engineering, Safety and Compliance

Every Vantage install is designed by a structural engineer licensed in the province, uses a Canadian-marked electric heater in a non-combustible envelope, and is permitted by the city before it goes on the roof. The governing codes are the BC Building Code 2024 in Victoria, the Vancouver Building By-law 2025 in Vancouver and the Ontario Building Code 2024 in Toronto, all built on the National Building Code 2020.

Structure. The galvanized steel base carries the module's weight (about 2,300 kg for Perch, 4,500 kg for Lodge and 8,000 kg for Summit, plus about 1,900 kg for a filled plunge) through adjustable feet and pads to the roof's beams and columns. Suites are designed for occupied roofs rated 4.8 kPa (Table 4.1.5.3); roofs rated lower are reinforced as site preparation. Vancouver and Victoria are in seismic category SC4, and the codes say friction may not resist earthquake forces on rooftop components. In BC the frame is therefore bolted to the structure with 4–8 engineered anchors that a roofer flashes. In Toronto a ballasted base with no roof penetrations can still pass where the site is SC1 or SC2 and the wind checks pass. The module roof takes 2.0 kPa of snow plus drift, and wind is checked to NBC 4.1.7.

Fire. All-electric heating with no combustion on the roof. Walls use non-combustible mineral wool and fibre-cement sheathing behind the cladding, and the roof is standing-seam steel. Most towers are of noncombustible construction, so for them we offer a noncombustible wood-look cladding (or a CAN/ULC-S134 test of the wood assembly) and ceiling boards that are fire-retardant treated or tested to a flame-spread rating of 25 or less. The module is sprinkler-ready with a high-temperature head, and a horn and strobe inside are tied to the building's fire alarm. The interior is capped at 90 °C. A sauna enclosure is not on the codes' list of rooftop structures left out of the storey count, so we get a code consultant's opinion before selling into 4–6 storey wood-frame buildings.

Electrical. Heaters carry a Canadian mark (cETLus, cULus or CSA) to CSA C22.2 No. 164-18; a US-only mark is not accepted. They are rated for 208 V three-phase, because a 240 V heater on 208 V gives only about 75% of its output. Buildings with only 347/600 V service get a 600 V to 208Y/120 V transformer kit. Wiring follows the Canadian Electrical Code, adopted as the BC Electrical Code and the Ontario Electrical Safety Code. Ontario's Rule 62-222 requires a timed cut-off of at most one hour with no override, so every Vantage heater has a hard-wired 1-hour timer and the app cannot extend a session. Electrical permits come from the City of Vancouver and the City of Victoria; in Ontario a licensed contractor files an ESA notification.

People safety. Smart-lock access only for booked users; an occupancy sensor; an emergency stop that cuts the heater and alerts the building; exterior signage on temperature, hydration and session length; a drinking-water and first-aid station; non-slip flooring, a 350 mm bench step and a guarded heater. The building sets session limits and age rules in Vantage Access.

Accessibility. Every suite has an 850 mm clear door (900 mm leaf) and a level or ramped entry. The Lodge and Summit hot rooms have a 1,500 mm turning space (CSA B651), and controls sit within reach. The lower bench has a transfer section, kept as best practice. The barrier-free route to the roof is the building's responsibility; Ontario requires one for rooftop amenity spaces. In BC an indoor shower must be accessible, which is an open design item for the Lodge vestibule.

Cold plunge. The Summit plunge and the Lodge add-on are regulated as pools in both provinces: a commercial pool that needs construction and operating permits in BC, and a Class B public cold plunge pool in Ontario. Each plunge has chlorine or bromine dosing with logging, a handrail, a clock, signs, an emergency stop and a health notice, and its permit costs are priced into the quote.

Permits. Building, electrical and plumbing permits; a development permit where Vancouver requires one; a health-authority permit for any plunge; and street-use permits for crane lifts. We keep one reusable permit drawing set for Vancouver, Victoria and Toronto, so each review starts from the same package.

Insurance and liability. Vantage carries general liability, products and completed-operations cover and an installation floater, and names the building owner as additional insured for the install. The building's own liability cover extends to the amenity in the same way as a gym or pool, and Vantage Care keeps the inspection record that insurers ask for.

Certification path. Before the first install we will have Canadian-marked heaters and an electrical field-evaluation path for the module. We then seek a listing of the whole module (heater, controls and room) to CSA C22.2 No. 164, plus UL 875 for export, and certify the workshop to CSA A277. The 2027 budget holds C$70,000 for Canadian certification and C$40,000 for heat, fire and load testing.

C. Frequently asked questions from building owners

Will my roof take the weight? Our suites are designed for occupied roofs rated 4.8 kPa, the code load for roofs people gather on, and the steel base carries the weight to the building's beams and columns. A licensed structural engineer reviews every site before we quote. If your roof is rated lower, we quote the reinforcement as site preparation.

Do you penetrate the roof? In Vancouver and Victoria, yes: seismic rules require the base to be bolted to the structure, so we use 4–8 engineered anchors that a roofer flashes under your roof warranty.
