Vantage

Seed round · Vancouver · November 2026

The amenity above everything.

Vantage designs, factory-builds and installs shared rooftop sauna suites for hotels, rental towers and condo buildings in Vancouver, Victoria and Toronto.

Vantage Sauna Co. · Confidential · Jacob [Last name], Founder & CEO

Open with the view. Every tower in Vancouver and Toronto has a roof it does nothing with, and every owner is looking for an amenity that sets the building apart. We put the two together. Vantage is a Canadian building-products company, not a spa operator: we design, build in the Fraser Valley, install and service rooftop sauna suites, and the building owns the amenity. We start in Vancouver and Victoria in 2027 and add Toronto in 2028.

The problem

Canadian towers compete on amenities. The roof sits empty.

New towers are hard to fill

59% of 2025 rental completions in the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area were still unleased at year end. New Metro Vancouver towers run 11.5–14.8% vacant.

Guests pay for sauna elsewhere

Vancouver has about 20 sauna studios charging C$24–55 a visit. Hotel guests and residents walk out of the building to use them.

The big options are out of reach

A rooftop pool or a spa floor needs plumbing, structure and space the building does not have. The roof stays a mechanical zone.

Today a rooftop sauna means an architect, a structural engineer, a sauna builder, an electrician, a crane company and several permits. No Canadian firm sells it as a product.

The solution

A rooftop sauna suite as a product: one contract, one price, 12 to 20 weeks.

All-electric. 208 V commercial heaters with a Canadian mark (cETLus, cULus or CSA). No gas, no flue. A transformer kit for 600 V buildings.

Factory-built in BC. Carried up the freight elevator as a flat-pack, or craned as a module. One to six days on site.

Engineered for the roof. Steel base for 4.8 kPa occupied roofs. Seismic anchors, flashed by a roofer, in BC; a ballasted base in Toronto where the site allows.

Run by software. Residents and guests book in the Vantage Access app; the smart lock and session timer do the rest.

Serviced for life. Vantage Care: quarterly inspections, heater stones, bench refinishing, 24-hour support.

03

We productized the custom project. The owner signs one contract at a from-price. We handle engineering, permits, the build in our Fraser Valley workshop, delivery and commissioning. In Canada the timeline is 12 to 20 weeks for a Perch or Lodge and 16 to 26 for a Summit, because permits, strata or condo votes and the pool permit for a plunge take longer than the build itself. In Vancouver and Victoria the base is bolted to the structure with engineered seismic anchors that a roofer flashes; the zero-penetration ballasted base applies only in Toronto, where the site allows it. In Ontario every heater has a hard-wired one-hour cut-off with no override, so the app books sessions inside that limit.

The nearest commercial product is a delivered-only cabin from about C$78,000; our Perch is about C$21,000 more installed, and that buys the structural and seismic check, permit drawings, the lift onto the roof, the electrical connection, commissioning and software. Why it is hard to copy

The product is the sauna. The moat is everything around it.

1. A seismic-ready rooftop kit

Steel base for 4.8 kPa occupied roofs, carried to beams and columns. In BC it bolts down with engineered, flashed anchors; in Toronto a ballasted base can pass on SC1/SC2 sites.

2. Canadian certification

Heaters marked cETLus, cULus or CSA to CSA C22.2 No. 164; 208 V design; Ontario’s hard-wired 1-hour timer. Then a CSA A277 factory and a module listing.

3. One permit package, three cities

Engineered once for BCBC, the Vancouver By-law and the Ontario code: 850 mm doors, CSA B651 turning space, a noncombustible cladding option, sprinkler-ready, pool permits for the plunge.

4. The installed base

Every building runs on Vantage Access and Care. Usage data, references and portfolio relationships compound with each roof.

The raise pays for this first: C$260,000 of engineering, certification and testing in 2027.

Vantage Sauna Co. · Seed round

10

None of these four is a patent. Seismic anchoring is the biggest design change for Canada: in Vancouver and Victoria the code says friction cannot resist earthquake forces on a rooftop component, so a ballast-only base does not pass, and a backyard sauna maker has no answer for that. A US-only ETL or UL mark is not enough in Canada; heaters need a Canadian mark, and buildings with only 600 V service need a transformer. CSA A277 is not mandatory, but in Ontario a certified module is deemed to comply with the code, which saves inspections. Together this is a year or more of engineering, certification and permitting that a competitor would have to repeat, plus the references and data that come only from installed buildings.
