In 2027 we spend about C$1.42M: C$665,000 of one-time launch costs and C$751,000 of running costs, against C$150,000 of gross profit from five pilot installs and C$60,000 of IRAP support. The biggest one-time item is engineering and Canadian certification: sealed seismic drawings for BC and a ballasted variant for Toronto, the 208 V and transformer design, the hard-wired timer, flame-spread tests on the aspen ceiling, and the start of the module listing and CSA A277 factory work. The chart is cash at the end of each quarter in the base plan. It falls while we build the product and the team, bottoms out at C$0.84M in the second quarter of 2029, and then rises as Toronto installs grow. That cushion covers slow permits, slow condo votes and a weaker year.
