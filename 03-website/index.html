How it works

From site assessment to first session in 12–20 weeks.

  1. 1

    Site assessment

    Free, with an answer in 48 hours. We check the roof's load rating, elevator or crane access, and whether the building has 208 V or only 600 V power.

  2. 2

    Structural and seismic engineering

    A structural engineer licensed in your province checks the roof and designs the base: engineered anchors in Vancouver and Victoria, ballast where a Toronto site allows it. No install without it.

  3. 3

    Approvals and permits

    A strata or condo vote where one is needed, then building, electrical and plumbing permits. A cold plunge adds a pool permit, and a crane lift a street-use permit. We prepare the drawings.

  4. 4

    Factory build

    4–6 weeks in our Fraser Valley workshop, while the permits are in review. Panels, glazing and heater are assembled and tested before they ship.

  5. 5

    Elevator or crane install

    1–6 days on site depending on model. A flat-pack kit by service elevator (the default for Perch and Lodge; no panel larger than 2.0 × 1.1 m), or a module by crane.

  6. 6

    Commissioning, training and launch event

    Heater commissioning, staff training, Vantage Access set-up, and a launch evening for your residents or guests.

Engineering and safety

Engineered for the roof, not adapted to it.

Designed for the BC Building Code, the Vancouver Building By-law and the Ontario Building Code. Every number below is in the specification we hand to your structural engineer, your insurer and your permit office.

  • Steel base frame, anchored for earthquakes

    Hot-dip galvanized frame on adjustable feet and rubber pads, designed for occupied roofs rated 4.8 kPa under the National Building Code and the BC and Ontario codes. Vancouver and Victoria are in Canada's highest seismic category, so in BC the frame is bolted to the structure with engineered anchors, flashed by a roofer. In Toronto, a ballasted base with no roof penetrations can pass where the site is seismic category SC1 or SC2 and the wind checks pass.

  • Canadian-marked heaters

    Commercial heaters rated for 208 V and marked cETLus, cULus or CSA to CSA C22.2 No. 164, wired to the Canadian Electrical Code. Each cycle ends on a hard-wired one-hour timer with no override, as Ontario requires, backed by an over-temperature cut-off and a door sensor. Buildings with only 347/600 V service get a step-down transformer kit.

  • Built for noncombustible towers

    Mineral-wool insulation (Euroclass A1), fibre-cement sheathing and a standing-seam steel roof. For towers of noncombustible construction we offer a noncombustible wood-look cladding, use tested ceiling boards (flame spread 25 or less), make the module sprinkler-ready with a high-temperature head, and tie a horn and strobe to the building alarm.

  • Accessible by design

    An 850 mm clear sauna door, a 1,500 mm turning space in the Lodge and Summit hot rooms (CSA B651), and a transfer section on the lower bench. The barrier-free route to the roof is the building's part; Ontario requires one for rooftop amenity spaces.

  • Factory certification planned

    We plan to certify our workshop to CSA A277, the Canadian procedure for factory-built buildings, so inspectors can accept concealed work without opening panels. In Ontario, an A277-certified module is deemed to comply with the building code; the installation is still inspected. We are also seeking a listing of the whole module, heater and room together, to C22.2 No. 164.

  • Cold plunge, run as a pool

    BC and Ontario both regulate a cold plunge as a pool. Ours comes with chlorine or bromine dosing and logging, a handrail, a clock, signs, an emergency stop and a health notice, and we prepare the health-authority permit application with you.

  • Access and safety

    Smart lock, occupancy sensor, emergency stop, and an interior temperature cap of 90 °C. Exterior safety signage, first aid and a drinking-water station.

FAQ

The questions owners ask first.

Will my roof take the weight?

Our suites are designed for occupied roofs rated 4.8 kPa, the load the National, BC and Ontario building codes set for roofs people gather on. Many existing roofs were designed only for maintenance and snow, which is far less. So a structural engineer licensed in your province reviews the roof and the point loads before we quote. If the roof needs reinforcement, we quote it as site preparation. We don't install without that review.

Do you drill into the roof?

In Vancouver and Victoria, yes, in a controlled way. Both cities are in Canada's highest seismic category, and the code does not let friction alone hold a rooftop structure in place during an earthquake. So the base frame is bolted to the building's structure with 4–8 engineered anchors, each flashed by a roofer. In Toronto, a ballasted base with no penetrations can pass where the site is seismic category SC1 or SC2 and the wind checks pass. Either way, the floor drain and roof gutter run to your existing roof drain.

What about fire code and insurance?

The walls use noncombustible mineral-wool insulation behind fibre-cement sheathing, under a standing-seam steel roof. Most towers are of noncombustible construction, so for them we use a noncombustible wood-look cladding, tested ceiling boards with a flame spread of 25 or less, a sprinkler-ready module with a high-temperature head, and a horn and strobe tied to the building alarm. Permits are required on every project; we prepare the permit set and walk your insurer through it.

Are the heaters approved for Canada?

We use only heaters that carry a Canadian mark (cETLus, cULus or CSA) to CSA C22.2 No. 164, rated for 208 V. A US-only ETL or UL mark is not enough in Canada. Every heater runs on a hard-wired one-hour timer with no override, as Ontario's electrical code requires, plus an over-temperature cut-off, and the interior is capped at 90 °C.

Our building has 600 V power. Is that a problem?

No, but it adds a step. Sauna heaters certified in Canada are rated 300 V or less, so a roof with only 347/600 V service needs a step-down transformer kit to 208 V. We quote it as site preparation, at C$10,000–25,000 depending on the run from the electrical room. The site assessment checks your supply first, so you know before you commit.

How long does it take?

12–20 weeks from signed contract to first session for Perch and Lodge, and 16–26 weeks for Summit. That covers engineering, any strata or condo vote, building and electrical permits, and the pool permit for a cold plunge. The factory build takes 4–6 weeks while permits are in review, and the on-site install 1–6 days depending on model.

How does a BC strata approve it?

A rooftop suite is a significant change to common property, so under the Strata Property Act it needs a 3/4 vote of the votes cast at a general meeting. Paying from the contingency reserve fund or by special levy needs a second 3/4 vote, and both resolutions can go on the same agenda. We supply an information-session kit, a structural summary and sample resolutions. Plan for 4–9 months from first conversation to the vote.

What about condos in Ontario?

Under Ontario's Condominium Act, a change that costs more than 10% of the annual budget needs owners of at least 66⅔% of all units to vote yes. Below that line, the board can go ahead after giving owners notice, unless owners of 15% of units call a meeting within 30 days and vote it down. In larger towers a Perch or Lodge can fall under the line. The reserve fund cannot pay for a new amenity, so we offer Vantage Flex. We start Toronto projects in 2028.

Do you charge GST, PST or HST?

Our prices are before tax. In Ontario we charge 13% HST. In BC we charge 5% GST, plus 7% PST where it applies; we are confirming with the BC Ministry of Finance how PST applies to a suite, and every quote shows it. Hotels and short-stay operators can usually recover GST/HST. Condo corporations and long-term rental landlords usually cannot, so we quote them with tax included.

Who maintains it?

Vantage Care does: quarterly inspections, heater stones, bench refinishing, parts, 24-hour support and plunge water logging, for C$450, C$700 or C$1,150 a month by model. Your staff don't need to touch the sauna beyond keeping the roof deck clear.

How do residents book it?

Through Vantage Access, at C$300 a month per building. Residents reserve a session, the smart lock opens for them at their time, and each booking starts one heater cycle of up to an hour. The app cannot extend a cycle past the timer. Managers see usage analytics.

Can hotels charge for it?

Yes. Vantage Access includes guest booking and session pricing, and Vancouver guests already pay: Fairmont Pacific Rim sells 45-minute sessions at C$40. For up to three lighthouse hotels we also offer a revenue-share pilot: C$0 upfront, Vantage takes 35% of session revenue, 5-year term, C$2,000 a month minimum.
