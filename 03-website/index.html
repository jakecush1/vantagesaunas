Engineering and safety

Engineered for the roof, not adapted to it.

Every number below is in the specification we hand to your structural engineer, your insurer and your permit office.

  • Steel base frame and ballast

    Hot-dip galvanised frame on adjustable feet and rubber pads, with ballast trays and parapet tie-back brackets. Designed for occupiable roofs rated 100 psf (4.8 kPa); the frame carries the load to the building's beams and columns, and a structural engineer reviews the point loads on every project.

  • Non-combustible build

    90 mm mineral-wool insulation (Euroclass A1), 12 mm fibre-cement sheathing and a standing-seam steel roof.

  • Commercial heater, ETL/UL 875 listed

    10.5 to 24 kW on 208–240 V three-phase, sized for the glass front, with a 60-minute session timer, over-temperature cut-off and door sensor.

  • Access and safety

    Smart lock, occupancy sensor, emergency stop, and an interior temperature cap of 90 °C (194 °F). Exterior safety signage, first-aid and a drinking-water station.

  • Zero roof penetrations

    Ballast and tie-back hold the module in place. The floor drain and the roof gutter run to your existing roof drain.

FAQ

The questions owners ask first.

Will my roof take the weight?

Our suites are designed for occupiable roofs rated 100 psf (4.8 kPa), the code load for roofs people gather on, and the steel base frame carries the weight to the building's beams and columns rather than spreading it across the deck. A structural engineer reviews the point loads on every site before we quote; if your deck is rated lower (many New York residential decks are 60 psf), we quote the reinforcement as site prep. We don't install without that review.

Do you penetrate the roof?

No. The base is ballasted and tied back to the parapet as the structural engineer specifies, on adjustable feet with rubber load-spreading pads. The floor drain and the roof gutter run to your existing roof drain, so nothing goes through the membrane.

What about fire code and insurance?

The walls use non-combustible mineral-wool insulation (Euroclass A1) behind fibre-cement sheathing, under a standing-seam steel roof. The heater is ETL/UL 875 listed with a 60-minute session timer and over-temperature cut-off, and the interior is capped at 90 °C (194 °F). Permits are required on every project; we prepare the design and permit set and walk your insurer through the package.

How long does it take?

6–10 weeks from signed contract to first session for Perch and Lodge, and 10–14 weeks for Summit. The factory build takes 4–6 weeks and the on-site install 1–6 days depending on model. Structural review and permits sit inside that window, and a slow permit office can stretch it.

Who maintains it?

Vantage Care does: quarterly inspections, heater stone replacement, bench refinishing, parts and 24-hour support, for $350, $550 or $900 a month by model. Your staff don't need to touch the sauna beyond keeping the roof deck clear.

How do residents book it?

Through Vantage Access, the booking app included at $249 a month per building. Residents reserve a session, the smart lock opens for them at their time, and the session timer runs the heater. Managers see usage analytics.

Can hotels charge for it?

Yes. Vantage Access includes guest booking and revenue tools, and hotels typically price sessions at $35–60 per guest. For a limited number of pilot hotels we also offer a revenue-share model: $0 upfront, Vantage takes 35% of session revenue, 5-year term, $1,500 a month minimum.

What if we have no three-phase power?

The heaters run on 208–240 V three-phase, so a new feed becomes part of site prep, which is billed separately at $8,000–$25,000 depending on the run. The 48-hour site assessment checks your electrical capacity first, so you know before you commit. In the buildings we assess, the question is usually the run to the roof rather than the service itself.
