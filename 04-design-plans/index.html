Sheets A-501 and A-502 · Details

Wall panel and base frame build-up

Shared across all three models. Layers are listed outside to inside; thicknesses are from the construction system spec.

Why this wall

A sauna wall has to do three jobs a normal wall does not: survive 90 °C and steam on the inside, stay non-combustible on a roof, and dry out every day. The thermo-aspen lining and the ventilated batten gap handle the heat and moisture; the aluminium-foil barrier (rated at least 120 °C, 0.1 perm or less) stops vapour reaching the mineral wool; the fiber-cement sheathing and Euroclass A1 wool keep the envelope non-combustible from the outside in; and the ventilated cavity behind the thermally modified cladding lets rain and sun do no harm. At roughly 185 mm it is thicker than a garden sauna wall because a rooftop sees wind, cold and fire exposure a garden does not.

Why this base

A roof membrane warranty, a landlord's patience and a structural engineer's sign-off all depend on the same thing: no holes, and no surprise loads. The hot-dip galvanized frame spreads the module onto adjustable feet that sit on rubber pads directly over beams and columns, so point loads land where the structure already carries them. Wind is resisted by weight and friction (ballast trays with pavers sized per site from ASCE 7-16 Section 29.5.1, with GCr of 1.9 lateral and 1.5 uplift) plus bolted tie-back brackets to the parapet, which take the lateral load on tall, exposed roofs where ballast alone would be too heavy. The grating sub-floor drains to a floor drain that runs to the building's roof drain, so the membrane under the module stays dry and inspectable.

Specification, all three models

Values are the v2 product spec. Electrical supplies are planning values for the building's electrical engineer; the final feeder size follows the listed heater's nameplate and NEC Articles 422 and 424. The selected model is highlighted.

Open items for the engineer of record (v2.1 reconciliation)

  1. Wall thickness vs footprint. The v2 brief's exterior footprints and interior room sizes net to 100 mm walls in plan (for example Lodge: 3.0 m outside, 2.8 m inside). The specified wall panel totals about 185 mm. This package draws the plans at the brief's room sizes with 100 mm nominal walls and shows the full 185 mm panel on sheet A-501. Either the footprint grows by about 170 mm per axis or the hot rooms shrink; decide before production drawings.
  2. Seat places vs headline ranges. With both tiers along the back wall and the ADA transfer section at the end of the lower tier, the drawn layouts seat 5 / 7 / 11 at 600 mm per place, or 7 / 9 / 13 at 550 mm, against the headline 4–6 / 8–10 / 12–14. Either restate the Lodge and Summit counts or lengthen those hot rooms by about 0.6 m.
  3. Summit effective volume. The brief's ~37 m³ was computed on a 3.4 × 3.4 m hot room. With the v2 room of 3.4 × 4.0 × 2.4 m and the full-length 4.0 × 2.4 m glass front the Harvia rule gives about 44 m³; confirm that 2 × 12 kW listed units on one controller cover it outdoors, or step up to 2 × 12.5 kW or 1 × 20 kW plus 1 × 12 kW. Perch and Lodge recompute to about 17 and 27 m³ and stay inside their heaters' ratings.
  4. Heater clearances. The Perch hot room cannot give the recommended 900 mm from heater to foot bench; the layout follows the listed heater's minimum clearances and guard. Confirm against the chosen heater's installation manual.
  5. Point loads. Publish the foot point loads, ballast mass and tie-back reactions per model once the frame is detailed; this package shows positions only.
  6. Lodge shower and lockers. The shower now sits inside the changing vestibule, which needs a wet-room floor and a 4.5 kW tank water heater on its own circuit; the lockers move outside as weatherproof units beside the entry. Neither change is in the $139,000 from-price yet.
  7. Tinted glass at night. A reflective tint only hides the interior while it is brighter outside. Confirm the coating, the night lighting scene and whether hotels want an interior blind.
Design notes

Why the suites are drawn this way

Short notes for the people who will build and sign off the suites, each grounded in the design research. Sources are named in brackets and listed below.

Two tiers, facing out

Both bench tiers run wall to wall along the back wall and face the glass, so every bather looks out over the parapet. The glass is the whole outside wall of the hot room; the two short side walls are solid thermo-aspen and carry the door and the heater corner, which keeps the heater off the glass and holds the heat. Seated on the upper bench, eyes are about 2.2 m above the roof surface, well above a typical 1 m parapet; the 3D model's Bench view shows that sightline.

Kirami FinVision and Iglucraft Panoramic product pages; Trumpkin's Notes

The law of löyly sets the bench height

Bathers' feet should sit above the stones and out of the cold lower third of the room, and the sitting bench should be 1,100 to 1,200 mm below the ceiling. The upper bench is at 1,150 mm, which leaves 1,150 mm of headroom under the 2.3 m Perch ceiling and 1,250 mm under the 2.4 m Lodge and Summit ceilings. That is why the exterior heights are 2.9, 3.0 and 3.1 m rather than the 2.6 to 2.8 m of the first draft.

Trumpkin's Notes; Finnleo construction guide (77 to 96 in ceilings)

Thermo-aspen inside, thermally modified ash or pine outside

Aspen is low in resin, stays cool to the touch and does not splinter; heat treatment makes it dimensionally stable in daily wet-dry cycles. Finnish guidance lists aspen, alder, abachi and linden for benches and advises against cedar in the hot room, which most North American kits still use. Kirami's commercial cabins use heat-treated aspen for the same reason.

Trumpkin's Notes; Kirami FinVision product page

Mechanical downdraft ventilation, and where the vents go

An electric heater does not pull air through the room the way a wood stove does, so the supply sits in the ceiling above the heater and a mechanical extract sits low on the back wall behind the benches. Design rate 9 to 12 L/s per bather at the full bench count: 54 to 72 L/s for Perch, 90 to 120 L/s for Lodge, 126 to 168 L/s for Summit. A CO₂ sensor (target below 700 ppm) drives the extract fan through Vantage Access.

Trumpkin's Notes; Finnleo construction guide

Privacy-tinted double glazing

The front wall is a tempered, double-glazed insulated unit with a bronze-tinted reflective outer pane and a low-iron inner pane in a thermally broken frame. By day the outer coating reads as a dark mirror from the roof while bathers see out. After dark, with the lights on inside, the effect reverses, so the night lighting scene keeps the hot room dim and an interior blind is an option. Harvia's sizing rule adds 1.2 m³ of heater volume for every square metre of glass, so the full-length front adds about 6, 8 and 12 m³; the brief's effective volumes of 16, 25 and 37 m³ recompute to about 17, 27 and 44 m³ for these rooms (open item 3).

Harvia electric heater catalogue (sizing rule); Kirami and Iglucraft product pages

A non-combustible envelope

A rooftop structure is exposed to wind-driven embers, neighbouring plant and the fire officer's questions. Fiber-cement sheathing, Euroclass A1 mineral wool and a standing-seam steel roof make every layer outside the foil non-combustible, while the thermally modified cladding is a thin rain screen on a ventilated cavity. Hotel rooftop precedents such as Das Weitzer in Graz went the same way with metal slats.

Research section 5, fire and clearances; Das Weitzer press release

No roof penetrations

Every bolt through a membrane is a leak and a warranty argument. The suites sit on a galvanized frame with ballast trays and parapet tie-back brackets instead. Wind on rooftop structures is checked to ASCE 7-16 Section 29.5.1 with GCr of 1.9 lateral and 1.5 uplift, which sets the ballast mass per site; the ballast is added to the roof load in the structural check.

STRUCTURE magazine, Wind loads on non-building structures; Roofing Magazine, wind loading rooftop equipment

Flat-pack first in dense cities

In New York a crane lift means DOB crane permits and inspections, DOT street permits, a licensed rigger and off-hours premiums. Perch and Lodge therefore ship as elevator-sized panel kits (panels no larger than 2.3 × 1.2 m, heaviest piece under 120 kg) and are assembled on the roof in one to three days. The craneable module is optional for those two and required for Summit, whose hot room and plunge do not fit a service elevator.

NYC DOB Cranes and Derricks permit page; Consigli, Hotel Indigo rooftop pool lift

Accessibility is designed in, not added

Where a building provides one sauna, that sauna must comply with the 2010 ADA Standards (241, 612). Each suite has an 813 mm (32 in) clear outward-swinging door with a 5 lbf opening force and a threshold no higher than 13 mm, and a 1,525 mm (60 in) turning circle inside the Lodge and Summit hot rooms (on the level landing for Perch, whose room is too narrow). The far end of the lower tier is a transfer section 1,070 mm long, 560 mm deep and 465 mm high, backed by the front of the upper tier; the lower-bench section beside it lifts out to give the 760 × 1,220 mm clear space at its end, as ADA 612.2 allows for a readily removable bench. The level route from the elevator to the door is the building's responsibility and a site-selection filter.

US Access Board, Chapter 6: Saunas and Steam Rooms

Listing the whole module to UL 875

UL 875 Edition 10 (2024), Electric Dry-Bath Heaters, covers "a heater unit combined with a prefabricated rigid room". Vantage pursues that configuration with an NRTL (UL or ETL) so the module, not just the heater, carries the mark. Wiring follows NEC Articles 422 and 424 and the listing instructions under 110.3(B), with a disconnect within sight of the heater or lockable.

UL Standards store, UL 875; Peak Saunas, 2023 NEC sauna requirements; Mike Holt forum threads

The 90 °C cap and the hygiene cycle

UL and ETL-listed heaters limit at about 194 °F (90 °C), so the controller caps the room there; the 100 °C figure in early drafts is not reachable with a listed unit. After the last booking of the day the controller holds 65 °C at the benches for 15 to 20 minutes to dry the wood before shutting down.

Haven of Heat, HUUM HIVE review; Trumpkin's Notes

Cold plunge sanitation and weight

The Summit plunge holds 1,200 L, three times a consumer tub, so it needs a 2 HP commercial chiller, ozone or AOP sanitation and 20 µm filtration, benchmarked on facility units such as Blue Cube's C3X and Modular. Filled it weighs about 1,900 kg and is placed over primary structure, never mid-span, which is why it sits on the terrace deck at the module's end.

Blue Cube Baths; Plunge technical specifications; Ice Barrel 500

Bearing design basis

The suites are designed for occupiable roofs rated 100 psf (4.8 kPa), the assembly and roof-garden live load in NYC Building Code Table 1607.1. Planning weight is at least 300 kg/m² dry, heavier than the 200 to 240 kg/m² of lighter insulated cabins on the market, plus ballast and about 90 kg per occupant. Roofs rated lower (60 psf residential decks, 20 psf ordinary roofs) need spreader frames or reinforcement, quoted as site preparation.

NYC Building Code 2022, Chapter 16 (UpCodes); research section 4, weight per m²

Seats, and how many people are comfortable

Bench seats are counted at 600 mm per place along the two tiers, which run 2.2, 2.8 and 4.0 m from side wall to side wall. The Finnish comfort guideline of 3 m³ per bather gives 4, 6 and 11 at full comfort for the three hot rooms, which is why the spec sheet shows both numbers. The Lodge and Summit upper benches are longer than 1.95 m, so a bather can lie down.

Trumpkin's Notes (Finnish RT card); Finnleo, 2 ft of upper bench per bather
