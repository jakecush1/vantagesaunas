## 1. US multifamily market size

**Totals and size bands (Census Bureau / HUD Rental Housing Finance Survey).**
- The 2024 RHFS counts **18,965,000 rental properties** and **49,722,000 rental units**. Properties with **50+ units number 81,000 (0.4% of properties) but hold 19,505,000 units (39.2% of all rental units)**. Properties with 25–49 units: 74,000 (2,708,000 units); 5–24 units: 330,000 (4,091,000 units). [Census 2024 RHFS infographic](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/programs-surveys/rhfs/2024-rhfs-infographic.pdf)
- The 2021 RHFS (2020 data), tabulated by the Congressional Research Service, breaks the large band down: **50–99 units: 15,000 properties (1,055,000 units); 100–149 units: 11,000 properties (1,296,000 units); 150+ units: 45,000 properties (16,387,000 units)**. Properties with 100+ units therefore total about **56,000**, holding **17.68M units (35.7% of all rental units)**; the 150+ band alone is 33.1% of rental units. Total 2020 stock: 19,328,000 properties / 49,547,000 units. [CRS R47332](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2022-12-13_R47332_c739bc53dd3dba8852c1194b08d1eb5f7a33f29c.html)
- The 2024 survey's 81,000 properties at 50+ units is consistent with the 2021 survey's 71,000 (15k + 11k + 45k), so the 100+ count has likely grown modestly from 56,000 since 2020 (new deliveries); no newer split was published.
- Ownership of large assets is institutional: LLP/LP/LLC entities own **67.8% of units in 100+ unit properties** (66.7% of 150+ properties); REITs own 4.4% of 150+ properties; individual investors only 4.4%. [CRS R47332](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2022-12-13_R47332_c739bc53dd3dba8852c1194b08d1eb5f7a33f29c.html)
- Economics of large properties: 2023 mean total operating expense was **$10,979 per unit** at 50+ unit properties (vs $6,325 at 25–49 units); median debt-service coverage ratio 164. [Census 2024 RHFS infographic](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/programs-surveys/rhfs/2024-rhfs-infographic.pdf)

**Class A share.** Not found on an opened page. NMHC's Quick Facts pages (which carry ACS tabulations of units by structure size and by metro) render only via JavaScript and returned no content. The NMHC 50 press release (search result, not opened) states the top-50 owners hold roughly 10% of US apartment stock; treat as unverified until opened.

**Rooftop amenity decks.** The only prevalence data found: Grace Hill's 2024 amenities study (HelloData website data from ~89,500 multifamily properties) lists **rooftop space, spa and multimedia center among the least common amenities that are still offered at more than 10% of properties**; fitness centers, pools and clubhouses are in more than a third of properties. The same study found clubhouses and pools "least correlated with a higher monthly rent... because many properties have these same amenities, renters are less willing to pay a premium," which is the core argument for a differentiated amenity. [Grace Hill 2024 Resident and Tenant Amenities Report](https://info.gracehill.com/hubfs/All%20GH%20Website%20File%20Downloads/Multifamily%20Ebooks/2024%20Resident%20and%20Tenant%20Amenities%20Report.pdf)

## 3. US office buildings

- **Count.** EIA's CBECS 2018 (released Sep 2021, revised Dec 2022) counts **970,000 office buildings**, of which **17,000 are 100,001–200,000 sq ft, 9,000 are 200,001–500,000 sq ft and 2,000 are over 500,000 sq ft — about 28,000 office buildings over 100,000 sq ft** (plus 35,000 at 50,001–100,000). Across all commercial building types there are 142,000 buildings over 100,000 sq ft. [EIA CBECS 2018 Table B6](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2018/bc/pdf/b6.pdf). No Class A split is published; CoStar/CBRE building-class counts were not obtainable.
- **Flight to quality (CBRE, 57 markets, 830 buildings).** "Prime" buildings are 2% of buildings and 8% of square footage but recorded **+48M sq ft net absorption from 2020 to mid-2024 while the other 92% lost 170M sq ft**; prime rents carried an **84% premium** in Q1 2024 (60% in 2018) and prime vacancy was 14.8%, 4.5 points below the rest of the market. [Bisnow, 25 Jun 2024](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/office/92-of-us-offices-struggle-to-keep-up-with-prime-amenity-rich-buildings-124849)
- **Amenity-specific premiums (JLL, Mar 2024).** Buildings with a **roof or sky terrace earn a 5.2% rent premium versus Class A buildings in the same submarket**; courtyard with outdoor seating +3.5%; LEED +2.8%; fitness center with showers +2.9% (fitness only +0.5%); food hall +1.4%; restaurant +0.1%. "Highly amenitized" buildings (10+ amenities plus a differentiated offering such as a roof terrace) gained 23.3M sq ft of occupancy since the pandemic while other urban Class A lost more than 50M sq ft. [Bisnow, 25 Mar 2024](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/office/office-amenities-tied-to-significant-rent-occupancy-growth-report-shows-123494) (JLL's own page now redirects; cite via Bisnow or retrieve the JLL PDF.)
- **National vacancy.** No national vacancy figure was read on an opened page (CoStar's 2025 office release returned 403). The CBRE prime/non-prime split above (14.8% vs ~19.3%) is the best opened proxy and is mid-2024.

## 4. Wellness economy, wellness real estate, sauna and cold plunge markets

- **Wellness real estate (GWI, May 2026):** $151B (2017) → $246B (2019) → $711B (2024) → **$876B (2025)**, forecast to pass $1T in 2027 and reach **$1.8T by 2030**. Growth averaged 23.6%/yr 2019–25 and is expected at 15%/yr 2025–30; 2024→25 growth was 23% versus 3% for global construction. **US market $254B in 2025**, 18%/yr growth 2019–25. [GWI press release](https://globalwellnessinstitute.org/press-room/press-releases/wellness-real-estate-market-reaches-876-billion-forecast-to-hit-1-8-trillion-by-2030/)
- **Earlier GWI edition (Build Well to Live Well, Jun 2025):** 2024 market $584B (headline) / $548B (body), 19.5%/yr growth 2019–24 vs 5.5% for construction, forecast ~$1.1T by 2029; the US is 41% of the market; wellness real estate is ~3.3% of global construction output. No price-premium figure was found in the opened articles. [Spa Business](https://www.spabusiness.com/wellness-news/Wellness-real-estate-market-forecast-to-double-to-US11-trillion-by-2029-according-to-GWI-report/356160). Note GWI restated 2024 upward in its 2026 release; cite the 2026 figures.
- **Wellness economy (GWI, Nov 2025):** **$6.8T in 2024**, +7.9% YoY, roughly doubled since 2013; forecast **$9.8T by 2029** at 7.6%/yr. Wellness real estate is the fastest-growing of 11 sectors (19.5% five-year CAGR; 15.2% forecast). [GWI blog](https://globalwellnessinstitute.org/global-wellness-institute-blog/2025/11/25/wellness-market-hits-record-6-8-trillion-will-reach-nearly-10-trillion-by-2029/)
- **Sauna market (Grand View Research):** **$954.2M (2025) → $1,556.9M (2033), 6.4% CAGR**; traditional saunas 42.79% of 2025 revenue; residential 58.92%; **commercial segment growing faster at 7.0% CAGR**; infrared 7.5%; **North America 28.06% share**; US 5.4% CAGR. Named players: Harvia Group, KLAFS, SAUNATEC, TyloHelo, Sauna360 (Masco), HUUM, Syracuse Sauna King, Effegibi, Nordic Sauna, Saunacore. [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/sauna-market-report). Caveat: this counts equipment, not installed-suite revenue; Vantage's ACV includes structure, install and service.
- **Cold plunge / contrast therapy:** no page opened. Search results (unverified) cluster around a **~$350M global cold-plunge-tub market in 2025 with 4–8% CAGR** (IMARC $350.3M; others $320–380M). No market-sizing of "contrast therapy" exists; the qualitative evidence is the contrast-therapy franchise wave (SweatHouz, Perspire) and the Bathhouse/Othership formats, which all pair sauna with cold plunge.

## 5. Evidence that wellness amenities drive rent, retention or hotel revenue

**Multifamily**
- NMHC/Grace Hill 2024 Renter Preferences Survey (172,703 verified renters): pools 76% interested (23% must-have); fitness centers similar; **hot tub or sauna: "more than half" interested, few consider it a necessity**; 93% of renters have an on-site fitness facility and ~55% use it at least monthly. [Multifamily Dive, 5 Feb 2024](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/apartment-renters-top-gym-features-crossfit-trx/705825/). Willingness-to-pay examples from the same survey: on-site childcare $59.31/mo, pet services $57.12, pickleball court $56.53 (among renters who would not rent without them). [Multifamily Dive, 27 Nov 2023](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/nmhc-survey-reveals-renters-top-preferences/700666/). No sauna-specific willingness-to-pay figure is public; the full report is paywalled at NMHC.
- Grace Hill 2024 amenities study: commodity amenities (pool, clubhouse) have the highest satisfaction but the lowest correlation with rent per sq ft "because many properties have these same amenities"; rooftop space and spa are offered by only >10% of properties (rare, hence differentiating). Rent model R² 0.56. [Grace Hill 2024 report](https://info.gracehill.com/hubfs/All%20GH%20Website%20File%20Downloads/Multifamily%20Ebooks/2024%20Resident%20and%20Tenant%20Amenities%20Report.pdf)
- No study quantifying a sauna/spa rent premium in multifamily was found; the office roof-terrace premium (5.2%, JLL) is the closest quantified analogue for rooftop amenity value.

**Hotels (CBRE Trends in the Hotel Spa Industry, 2024 data, 297 US hotels with spas, avg 381 rooms, 65.1% occupancy, ADR $375.64)**
- Spa revenue per available room **$6,061** (luxury $9,847; upper-upscale $3,197; resort $6,539; urban $4,756); spa = **3.4% of total hotel revenue** (luxury 4.2%, resorts 3.5%). Spa revenue +1.4% in 2024 (luxury +3.6%, urban +4.1%); profit −0.5% as labour rose 3.9%. **Memberships and day packages drove the largest revenue gains**; product sales are 15–30% of spa revenue. [Spa Business](https://www.spabusiness.com/spa-business-magazine/Research-Marginally-speaking/37870). No ADR-uplift-from-wellness figure was found on an opened page.
- Implication for the revenue-share model: an upper-upscale urban hotel with 200 rooms generates roughly $0.6–1.0M/yr in spa revenue at CBRE's PAR benchmarks; a rooftop sauna selling sessions at the brief's $35–60 price point is a low-labour addition to that line (estimate, label as such).

**Office**
- Roof terrace +5.2% rent, courtyard +3.5%, full-service fitness +2.9% (JLL via Bisnow); prime buildings +84% rent and −4.5 pts vacancy (CBRE via Bisnow). See section 3.

## 7. Competitors and adjacent players

- **Harvia (Finland, listed; owns Almost Heaven Saunas, US).** Harvia bought Almost Heaven's business in Dec 2018 for ~EUR 4M debt-free; Almost Heaven revenue grew from $3.5M (2014) to ~$9M (2017) with "somewhat negative" EBITDA; Harvia's North American revenue was only ~EUR 3M in 2017. Manufacturing in Renick, WV; sales in Holland, MI; positioned as the base for a broader North American offering. [Harvia release, 21 Dec 2018](https://harviagroup.com/harvia-strengthens-its-position-in-the-united-states-by-acquiring-the-business-of-almost-heaven-saunas-llc/). ## 8. Regulatory and cost benchmarks

- **Roof structure and permitting (NYC-focused guide, May 2026):** NYC residential rooftop decks must carry a **60 psf live load** (accessory decks at 1.5× the primary live load); IBC minimum is 40 psf for occupied recreational roofs; Chicago requires 100 psf for private residential roof decks; a **filled hot tub often exceeds 100 psf**; 2-inch concrete pavers add 11–13 psf. Costs: decking surface **$30–85/sq ft installed** (25–40% of total project), roof access bulkhead $10–25k+, membrane replacement $6–18k, **structural reinforcement "rarely under $15,000,"** permit fees $500–3,000. NYC DOB Alt-1 filings (change of use/occupancy/egress) add **3–6 months** and require an amended Certificate of Occupancy; **Place of Assembly** is triggered at 75+ occupants; Landmarks review applies in historic districts. [Block Renovation](https://www.blockrenovation.com/guides/building-a-rooftop-deck-costs-structure-decisions)
  - Implication for the brief: the brief assumes siting on decks rated ≥100 psf. Many existing NYC residential roof decks are designed to 60 psf, so the Summit's filled plunge (1,900 kg over a small footprint) and crowd loading need a per-site structural check; the ≤50 psf module target is compatible with 60 psf decks only if occupant live load is handled in the engineer's combination.
- **Crane and rigging:** no NYC day-rate or street-closure cost was read on an opened page (Statista's Turner & Townsend city comparison is paywalled; BigRentz generic pages quote $200–$1,000/day for small-to-large mobile cranes but were not opened). Gap: obtain two NYC rigger quotes.
- **Electrical service upgrades:** no sourced benchmark found. The brief's $8,000–$25,000 site-prep allowance remains an assumption.
- **City programs for rooftop amenities:** none found. (NYC's sustainable-roof laws for new roofs/roof replacements should be checked as a constraint; not researched here.)
- **Operating context:** large rental properties spend $10,979 per unit per year in operating expense (2023), so a $8,400/yr Care + Access subscription equals <1% of opex at a 100-unit property. [Census 2024 RHFS](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/programs-surveys/rhfs/2024-rhfs-infographic.pdf)
