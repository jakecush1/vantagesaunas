Canada: codes, engineering and permitting for Vantage rooftop sauna suites

Prepared 1 Nov 2026 for the Canadian launch: Vancouver (hotels, business/serviced apartments, purpose-built rental towers, strata condos), Victoria, and Toronto (hotels, rental towers, condominium corporations). It checks the current US/NYC spec in BRIEF.md section 2 against the codes that apply in British Columbia and Ontario, and lists what has to change.

Bottom line (read this first)

  1. The "zero roof penetrations, ballast only" base does not meet code in Vancouver or Victoria. BCBC 2024 and the Ontario code (both built on the National Building Code 2020) say that for rooftop components, "friction due to gravity loads shall not be considered to provide resistance to earthquake forces". The only exception is ballasted solar arrays on roofs that are not normally occupied. Both cities are in the top seismic category, SC4. My estimate of the design sideways earthquake force on a rooftop module is about 0.8 to 1.1 times its own weight in Vancouver and 1.5 to 1.8 times in Victoria, at minimum. Adding ballast raises that force, because the force is proportional to weight. The base frame has to be bolted to the building's structure. In Toronto, a ballasted base may still pass seismic checks if the engineer classes the module as equipment and the site is in seismic category SC1 or SC2. Wind still has to be checked.
  2. Heaters must carry a Canadian mark. Acceptable marks are cETLus, cULus or CSA, to CSA C22.2 No. 164-18 or CSA E60335-2-53. A US-only "ETL" or "UL" mark is not enough. C22.2 No. 164 covers heaters rated 300 V or less, so in buildings that only have 347/600 V service a step-down transformer is needed. In 120/208 V buildings, Vantage must specify heaters rated for 208 V. A 240 V heater run on 208 V gives about 75% of its rated output.
  3. Ontario's electrical code requires a 1-hour timed cut-off with no override on every sauna heater (OESC Rule 62-222). This limits how far the Vantage Access app can schedule or extend sessions remotely. Get a written interpretation before launch.
  4. Accessibility rules are stricter than ADA on dimensions and looser on sauna detail. Doors must give 850 mm clear, not the 813 mm in the current spec. Universal washrooms and dressing rooms use a 1,700 mm turning circle. Ontario explicitly requires a barrier-free path to "rooftop amenity spaces". In BC, if a building provides showers (outside a suite), each group of showers must include an accessible one. That affects the Lodge's indoor shower. Neither code has a sauna clause like ADA 612, so keep the ADA transfer bench as best practice.
  5. A cold plunge is a regulated pool in both provinces. Ontario now defines a "public cold plunge pool" (kept at 15 °C or less and not drained before each user). At condos and apartments with 6 or more units, and at hotels, it is a Class B pool, with 5–10 ppm free chlorine, a handrail, a clock, signs and 14 days' notice to public health before opening. In BC a 1,200 L plunge at a hotel, strata or membership business is a "commercial pool" if it is 61 cm deep or more. It needs a health-authority construction permit (plans sealed by an engineer or architect) and an annual operating permit. Saunas themselves are not regulated by public health in either province.
  6. Fire rules for high-rises. Most towers must be built of noncombustible construction. The thermally modified wood cladding is then allowed only if the building is sprinklered throughout and the wall assembly has passed the CAN/ULC-S134 fire test. Wood ceiling finishes inside need a flame-spread rating of 25 or less. In a sprinklered building the module will probably need a high-temperature sprinkler. A sauna enclosure is not on the list of rooftop enclosures that are left out of the storey count, so in some buildings it could add a storey.
  7. Snow is heavier in Vancouver than in Toronto. The basic roof snow load is 1.64 kPa at Vancouver City Hall and 1.12 kPa at Toronto City Hall. Occupied roofs take the 4.8 kPa assembly live load in both provinces, the same as the 100 psf NYC figure in the brief.
  8. Timelines are longer than the US 6–10 weeks. In Ontario the city must decide a complete permit application within 20 business days for Part 3 buildings, or 30 for high-rises. Vancouver's median for commercial renovation permits was 32 days in mid-2024. Strata or condo approval, a pool permit and street-use permits are added on top. My estimate is 12–20 weeks from contract to first session for Perch and Lodge, and 16–26 weeks for Summit.

1. Governing code, permits, factory certification, fire rules, timelines

1.1 Which code governs a factory-built rooftop sauna on an existing building

  • It is building work on an existing Part 3 building. Hotels, rental towers and condo towers are almost always "Part 3" buildings: over 600 m² footprint, over 3 storeys, or containing assembly use. The new enclosure must meet the current code (BCBC 2024, VBBL 2025 or OBC 2024). Existing parts of the building generally only need upgrading where the new work triggers it.
  • Ontario puts this in Part 11 (Renovation). New work "shall comply with all other Parts", and the existing building's "performance level" must not be reduced. If a change of use raises the live load and the existing roof framing "is not adequate", remedial work is required, or the area must be load-restricted and signed. OBC 2024 Part 11, 11.3.1.2, 11.4.2.1, 11.4.3.2 [code text read]
  • Ontario's Part 11 hazard-index table lists "Public Saunas" under Group D (business and personal services). A hotel sauna sold to outside guests could be read as a Group D use, which matters for change-of-use permits. OBC Part 11, Table 11.2.1.1.-J [code text read]
  • In BC, accessibility upgrades for alterations apply "where providing such access would be practical" (3.8.4.5). Vancouver has its own Part 11 for upgrades to existing buildings, which I could not read. BCBC 2024 3.8.4 [code text read]; VBBL Part 11 draft change 24-0030 [search excerpt only]
  • Zoning is a separate gate. Vancouver's Zoning and Development By-law lets the Director of Planning approve "common roof-top amenity structures … that do not exceed a height of 3.6 m" above the height limit. That is discretionary, so expect a development permit step. Perch, Lodge and Summit are 2.9–3.1 m tall, which fits. Vancouver Zoning By-law s.10 [search excerpt only; unverified on fact-check 2026-11-01, 403]. Toronto's zoning by-law 569-2013 lets unenclosed rooftop amenity structures exceed the height limit (3.0 m in commercial zones). Recent site-specific by-laws allow 4–5 m for trellises and wind screens. An enclosed sauna is not clearly covered and may need a minor variance. Toronto zoning amendments, Bill 645 (2026) [search excerpt only]

1.3 Does a factory-built unit need CSA A277 certification?

It is not mandatory in either province. In practice it is worth getting. - CSA A277 ("Procedure for certification of prefabricated buildings, modules, and panels") is a factory quality and inspection procedure, "not a building code". BCBC 2024 says the code "applies the same requirements to site-built and factory-constructed buildings". If a unit carries an accredited agency's A277 label, "the accepting authority will have some assurance that the concealed components do not require re-inspection on site". Without the label, inspectors can ask to open up panels or inspect in the factory. BCBC 2024 Note A-1.1.1.1.(3) [code text read] - Ontario goes further. "A manufactured building or a manufactured part of a building within the scope of Part 3 is deemed to comply with this Code if it is certified in accordance with CSA A277." Site installation is still inspected. OBC 2024 Division C 1.12.1.1 [code text read]. A Toronto staff report notes that inspectors no longer have to visit the factory for A277-certified modules. Toronto staff report, 2025 [search excerpt only]. The OBC references the 2016 edition (CSA A277-16). OBC Div B Table 1.3.1.2 [code text read] - A277 covers the building side only. The electrical side (heater plus wiring plus room) needs its own certification or a field evaluation (section 3). - Recommendation: certify the Vantage factory and the panel kit to A277 through an SCC-accredited body such as QAI, Intertek or CSA, scoped to NBC 2020, BCBC 2024, VBBL 2025 and OBC 2024. Cost was not found; budget CAD 20–50k first year plus audits (estimate, based on the scope of a factory audit program; get quotes).

1.4 Fire and combustibility rules for rooftop structures

  1. The enclosure may count as a storey. Only rooftop enclosures for elevator machinery, a service room, an access/egress stair or an elevator lobby are excluded "in calculating the building height". BCBC 2024 3.2.1.1.(1) [code text read]. Ontario's list is shorter still: elevator machinery, a stairway, or a service room "used for no purpose other than for service to the building". OBC 3.2.1.1.(1) [code text read]. A sauna is not on either list. On a 6-storey wood-frame rental building (common in Vancouver and Victoria) a sauna could make it "7 storeys" and break the combustible-construction limits. Get a code consultant's opinion or an alternative solution before selling into 4–6 storey wood buildings.
  2. Noncombustible host buildings (most towers). A part of a building required to be noncombustible must use noncombustible materials, except for the listed allowances. BCBC 2024 3.1.5.1 [code text read] - Exterior wood cladding is allowed only if the building is 3 storeys or less or sprinklered throughout, and the wall assembly passes CAN/ULC-S134: flaming no more than 5 m above the opening, and heat flux no more than 35 kW/m² at 3.5 m. Fire-retardant-treated wood must be tested after accelerated weathering. BCBC 3.1.5.5 [code text read]. The current thermally modified ash cladding has no S134 test, so on towers use noncombustible cladding (aluminium or steel profiles, fibre cement) or fund an S134 test. - Interior finishes: wall finishes up to 25 mm thick with a flame-spread rating of 150 or less are allowed. Ceiling finishes up to 25 mm thick need a flame-spread rating of 25 or less, with up to 10% of the ceiling allowed at 150, unless they are fire-retardant-treated wood. BCBC 3.1.5.12 [code text read]. The 15 mm thermo-aspen ceiling almost certainly does not reach 25 (estimate: untreated softwood and hardwood boards usually test well above 25). Options: CAN/ULC-S102 testing of the actual boards, a fire-retardant-treated ceiling (an odour and off-gassing question in a 90 °C room), or an alternative solution. - Mineral wool and fibre-cement sheathing are already noncombustible. Foil vapour barriers count as minor components. BCBC 3.1.5.2 [code text read]
  3. Sprinklers. NBC-based codes call up NFPA 13 for sprinklered buildings. NFPA 13 "generally requires sprinklering of any concealed spaces of combustible construction or where large amounts of combustibles are present". BCBC Note A-3.1.11.5.(3) [code text read]. Fire protection engineers report that saunas in sprinklered buildings are sprinklered, typically with 286 °F (141 °C) heads. MeyerFire forum, Mar 2024 [page opened]. NFPA 13 classes 250–300 °F heads as "High" (225 °F maximum ceiling temperature) and 325–375 °F as "Extra High" (300 °F ceiling). NFPA 13 Table, via NFPA first revision report [search excerpt only]. Design change: a sprinkler-ready module (dry-pendent high-temperature head in the hot room, standard head in the vestibule, a stub for connection to the building's system, freeze protection for Toronto's −20 °C design temperature).
  4. People on the roof trigger exit rules. "Means of egress shall be provided from every roof which is intended for occupancy". The exit at roof level must be "designed in conformance with the requirements for exits", which in practice means an exit stair, not a hatch. Two exits are required if the roof's occupant load is more than 60. A rooftop enclosure that is not itself an exit needs an access to exit leading to an exit at roof level or on the storey below. BCBC 3.3.1.3.(3)–(9) [code text read]
  5. Fire alarm signals must be "clearly audible … throughout any occupancy on a roof". BCBC 3.2.4.18 [code text read]. This rules out a module that blocks alarm sound (add a horn and strobe inside, tied to the building system).
  6. Guards of at least 1,070 mm are required "around any roof to which access is provided for purposes other than maintenance". They must be non-climbable between 140 and 900 mm where the drop is more than one storey. BCBC 3.3.1.18 [code text read]. Many existing parapets are lower, so this is site preparation.
  7. Sauna fire history in Ontario. The Fire Marshal and ESA recorded 3 sauna-fire deaths in 10 years. They name the main causes as combustibles left in the room, missing or bypassed high-limit switches and thermostats, unapproved replacement parts, and heaters too close to combustibles. ESA Flash 25-20-FL [page opened]. This supports Vantage Care's tamper-evident controls and parts-only-from-Vantage policy.

What NBC 2020 / BCBC 2024 / OBC 2024 Article 4.1.8.18 requires [code text read in both codes]: - Rooftop "machinery, fixtures, equipment and tanks (including contents)" (Category 11) and "penthouses" (Category 5) are designed for a lateral force Vp = 0.3 · S(0.2) · IE · Sp · Wp. - Sp = Cp·Ar·Ax/Rp, between 0.7 and 4.0. - At roof level the height factor is Ax = 1 + 2·hx/hn = 3.0. - Equipment is assumed "flexible or flexibly connected" unless shown to have a period of 0.06 s or less. That gives Cp 1.0, Ar 2.5, Rp 2.5, so Sp = 3.0. If proven rigid: Ar 1.0, Rp 1.25, so Sp = 2.4. - Connections "shall be designed to support the component … for gravity loads … and … friction due to gravity loads shall not be considered to provide resistance to earthquake forces" (4.1.8.18.(7)(a) in both BCBC and OBC; OBC sentence number corrected on fact-check 2026-11-01). - The only exception is a ballasted array of interconnected solar panels where IE·S(0.2) is 1.0 or less. It applies only if "the roof is not normally occupied" and the parapet, height, slope, clearance and tested-friction conditions are all met (4.1.8.18.(17)–(18)). An occupied amenity roof can never use it. - Post-installed anchors in concrete must be pre-qualified for seismic use by cyclic testing (CSA A23.3; ACI 355.2 or 355.4). Shallow anchors use Rp = 1.5. Adhesive or power-actuated (non-ductile) connections use Rp = 1.0, and power-actuated fasteners are not allowed for cyclic tension. - For SC1 and SC2 buildings, Sentence (1) "need not apply to Categories 6 through 22" (equipment, components). It does still apply to Category 5 (penthouses). In Toronto, how the engineer classes the module decides whether it needs seismic restraint. - Glass: the glazing must not fall out at 1.25 × the design drift (or 13 mm). This does not apply in SC1/SC2, or to fully tempered monolithic glass in non-post-disaster buildings within 3 m of a walking surface (4.1.8.18.(14)–(15)). The full-length tempered IGU front needs its frame clearance checked in BC. - Guidance for nonstructural seismic restraint is in CSA S832. BCBC Note A-Table 4.1.8.18 [code text read]

Arithmetic: Vp/Wp = 0.3 × S(0.2) × 1.0 × 3.0. Upper values (S(0.2) = 1.5 × Smax) are 50% higher. If the module is proven rigid, multiply by 0.8. Ballast that rides with the module adds to Wp and so adds to Vp.

Is a ballast-only, non-penetrating base acceptable? - Vancouver and Victoria: no. The code bars counting friction, and the solar exception excludes occupied roofs. The base frame must be positively connected to structure that can take forces about equal to (Vancouver) or about twice (Victoria) the module's weight. - Toronto: possibly, if the engineer classes the module as Category 11 or 18–21 equipment and the site is SC1 or SC2. Wind and overturning still have to be resisted, by ballast, tie-backs or both. If the building official treats it as a Category 5 "penthouse", Vp of about 0.22 × weight applies and friction cannot be counted.

2.5 What this means for the steel base frame

  1. Make two base variants: - "BC seismic base" (Vancouver, Victoria, any SC3/SC4 site): 4–8 structural posts or pedestals per module, bolted to the slab or beams with seismically pre-qualified anchors. Each post gets a factory-flashed curb compatible with common membranes (SBS, PVC, TPO). Base members are sized for Vp at the upper bound (up to about 2.7 × weight in Victoria on soft soil), plus overturning. - "Ontario ballast base" (SC1/SC2, engineer agrees it is Category 11 or 18–21): the current ballast trays plus parapet tie-back, checked for wind sliding and uplift.
  2. Drop "zero roof penetrations" from BC marketing. Say "a small number of engineered, flashed structural anchors, coordinated with the roofer to keep the membrane warranty".
  3. Parapet tie-backs count as seismic connections in BC. They must be designed for the full share of Vp. The parapet itself must be checked; existing parapets are rarely designed for 35–140 kN point loads.
  4. Ballast is the enemy in BC. Every kilogram of ballast moving with the module adds earthquake load. Use it only where it serves wind uplift and the anchors can take the extra seismic mass.
  5. Plunge tank: anchor the tank as a "tank including contents" (Category 11) with the water mass included. Check sloshing, and the 10%-of-floor-mass rule in 4.1.8.18.(5).
  6. Glazing: specify frame clearances so the IGU does not fall out at the code drift, or use a laminated inner lite, on BC jobs.
  7. Drift accommodation: pipe, drain and conduit connections from the building to the module need flexible joints sized for building drift.
