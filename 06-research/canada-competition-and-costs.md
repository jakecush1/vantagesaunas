Compiled 1 Nov 2026 for the scaled-down Canadian plan (Vancouver first, then Victoria and Toronto; targets: hotels, serviced / corporate / furnished apartments and purpose-built rental towers, and condos — strata in BC, condominium corporations in Ontario).

## Summary: what should drive the Canadian plan

1. **Nobody in Canada sells what Vantage sells.** Canadian sauna makers either ship residential kits and barrels (about CAD 8,000–15,000) or deliver a commercial cabin with no install, structure or rooftop scope (Fritz Tiny Homes, Alberta: from CAD 77,850 and CAD 79,150, delivered only). No firm found offers a turnkey rooftop suite with engineering, crane or electrical, and only one Canadian rooftop sauna on a commercial building was found (Hampton Inn & Suites, downtown Vancouver, from travel listings). That is white space, but also proof that the market is unproven.
2. **The real competition for hotels is the social-sauna boom.** Othership (Toronto, CAD 58 a session in Mar 2026), Tevah (downtown Vancouver, 725 sq ft sauna for 60 people), HAVN (floating sauna in Victoria's Inner Harbour; Vancouver barge planned for mid-2027) and smaller operators charging CAD 35–45. Guests can walk to these. A hotel needs a reason to own one on its roof.
3. **Labour is cheaper than in the NYC plan.** Job Bank medians (wages updated 19 Nov 2025): carpenter CAD 31.50/h in the Lower Mainland and 36.00/h in Toronto; construction electrician 34.57 and 34.86; construction PM 51.92 and 51.28; structural engineer 49.74 and 51.28; technical sales 41.03 and 40.06. A fully loaded office hire costs roughly CAD 100–130k, against the brief's USD 115k (CAD ~161k).
4. **A factory in the Fraser Valley costs about CAD 21–24 per sq ft per year gross.** CBRE Q1 2026: Abbotsford 17.25 net + 5.45 taxes and operating costs (T&O); Chilliwack 15.32 + 5.48; Langley 18.15 + 5.24; Maple Ridge/Pitt Meadows 17.28 + 4.87; Vancouver proper 20.40 + 8.92. Metro asking rents fell to 18.87 in Q3 2026 (5.0% vacancy), so tenants have bargaining power. The GTA is cheaper on net rent (CAD 16.06 average, Q3 2026) but tight (2.6% vacancy).
5. **Use 1.40 CAD per USD.** Bank of Canada: 1.3960 on 30 Oct 2026, October average about 1.41, range 1.35–1.43 so far in 2026. EUR/CAD was 1.5582.
6. **Tariffs push us to build in Canada and buy heaters from the EU.** Sauna heaters (tariff item 8516.29.00) pay 7% MFN duty but are **duty-free from the EU, UK, US and Mexico**. However, Canada's Sept 2026 counter-tariffs hit some US-origin goods at 15/25/50% (US softwood lumber at 25%, many refrigeration items at 25%, US steel and aluminium raised to 50%). A 25% surtax on steel "derivative" goods (including prefabricated structures and building frames) applies to imports from all countries. Buying Canadian steel and an EU-origin heater avoids most of this.
7. **GST/HST is a real cost for condos and rental towers.** Hotels and short-stay operators can recover GST/HST. Residential condo corporations and long-term residential landlords generally cannot, so a Toronto condo pays 13% HST on top of the price and a BC strata pays at least 5% GST. Whether BC's 7% PST applies to the full contract depends on whether the ballasted, removable module is legally "tangible personal property" or an "improvement to real property". **Get a written PST ruling before quoting in BC.**
8. **Condo governance sets the sales cycle.** In BC, a 3/4 owner vote is needed for a special levy, for most spending from the reserve fund and for a significant change to common property. In Ontario, a change costing more than 10% of the annual budget is "substantial" and needs a yes vote at a meeting from owners of **at least 66⅔% of all units** (not of votes cast; Condominium Act s. 97(4)); below that, the board can proceed after giving notice unless owners of 15% of units requisition a meeting within 30 days and vote it down. A Perch (about CAD 112k with HST) can fall under the threshold in a mid-size Toronto building; a Lodge usually will not.
9. **Hotels set aside about 4–5% of revenue for furniture, fixtures and equipment (FF&E).** A 200-room downtown Vancouver hotel (2025 RevPAR CAD 223) holds roughly CAD 0.9M a year in reserve [E], so a Lodge is about one-fifth of one year's reserve.
10. **Canadian seed rounds average CAD 3–4.5M.** CVCA: CAD 3.00M average seed round in H1 2025 and CAD 4.48M in Q1 2026. A CAD 3.0–3.5M seed plus CAD 0.5–1.0M non-dilutive would be a normal Canadian round for the scaled-down plan.
11. **Non-dilutive money is real but slow.** SR&ED refunds 35% on up to CAD 6M of qualifying R&D a year (enacted 26 Mar 2026). NRC IRAP funds R&D staff for firms with up to 500 staff. PacifiCan offers interest-free repayable loans of CAD 200k–5M, but its intake is closed. CSBFP backs bank loans up to CAD 1.15M, but equipment and leasehold improvements are capped at CAD 500k of that. Futurpreneur offers up to CAD 75k (founder aged 18–39). BC investors get a 30% tax credit for investing in an Eligible Business Corporation.
12. **Proposed CAD list ("from", installed, before tax):** Perch **CAD 99,000**, Lodge **CAD 179,000**, Summit **CAD 329,000**. That is 6–9% below the US list after conversion. It gives 43–45% gross margin if Canadian costs in CAD come to 1.2 times the US cost figures in USD, and 38–41% if they come to 1.3 times [E]. In the launch year, lead with Perch and Lodge; quote Summit to order, because downtown crane lifts add CAD 15–35k [E].

Takeaways
- The Canadian benchmark for a commercial-grade cabin is **about CAD 78–79k delivered, with nothing installed**. Vantage's Perch at CAD 99k installed (section 5) is about CAD 20k above that, and the CAD 20k buys the structural check, permit drawings, roof lift, electrical connection, commissioning and software.
- Residential barrels at CAD 8–15k are what a cost-cutting strata council will compare us to. The answer is commercial duty: a commercial-rated heater, CSA certification, an accessible bench, a smart lock and a service plan.
- Custom builders (Vancouver Sauna, SaunaFin) are the likely local alternative for a hotel that wants a sauna inside an existing spa or amenity floor. They are not set up for rooftops.

2. **Ask the BC Ministry of Finance for a written PST interpretation.** The module rests on ballast and tie-backs with no roof penetrations, but it is hard-wired and plumbed. ### 5.3 What condo boards, rental owners and hotels can spend

**Ontario condominium corporations**
- A change to common elements whose total estimated cost exceeds **10% of the annual budgeted common expenses** (or that the board elects to treat as substantial) is "substantial" and needs a yes vote from owners of **at least 66⅔% of all units of the corporation**, taken at a meeting called for the purpose (s. 97(4)–(6)). This counts all units, not votes cast, so owners who do not vote count against. Below 10%, the board can proceed after sending a notice; owners of at least 15% of units can requisition a meeting within 30 days, and the change stops only if that meeting votes it down (s. 97(3), s. 46(1)). Changes up to the greater of CAD 1,000 or 1% of the budget a month need no notice (s. 97(2)) ([Condominium Act, 1998, e-Laws Word text, consolidated from 31 Dec 2025](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/docs/98c19_e.doc) [opened]; [REMI Network on s. 97](https://www.reminetwork.com/?p=3959) [opened; 2013]).
- GTA condo fees run roughly **0.60–1.50 per sq ft per month** depending on age and amenities (blog and broker estimates) ([TenantPay, 2026](https://www.tenantpay.com/blog/toronto-condo-fees-explained) [snippet]).
- Worked example [E]: 200 units x 750 sq ft x 0.90 per sq ft per month = **CAD 1.62M annual budget**, so the 10% threshold is **CAD 162k**. A Perch (CAD 111,870 with HST) fits under it; a Lodge (CAD 202,270) needs an owner vote unless the building is about 250+ units, and that vote needs yes votes from owners of at least 134 of the 200 units. **Lead Ontario condo sales with Perch, and offer Flex leasing so the cost shows up as an operating line.** Caution: s. 97(6) measures the change's total cost regardless of which fiscal year it falls in, so the 84-month lease total probably still counts toward the 10% test [E; confirm with a condo lawyer].

**BC strata corporations**
- A **3/4 vote** at a general meeting is needed to approve a special levy, to spend from the contingency reserve fund (only a majority vote if the depreciation report recommends the expense; no vote in an emergency) and for a significant change to the use or appearance of common property ([BC Gov: types of strata voting](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/meetings-and-voting/types-of-voting) [opened]; [BC Gov: special levies](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances-and-insurance/special-levies) [snippet]).
- Downtown Vancouver strata fees are about **0.62–0.78 per sq ft per month** (realtor estimate; amenity-heavy new towers above 0.80–1.00) ([Mike Stewart](https://www.mikestewart.ca/strata-fees/) [snippet]).
- Worked example [E]: 150 units x 750 sq ft x 0.70 = **CAD 945k a year**. Every Vantage purchase will need a 3/4 vote, so a strata sale means a 6–12-month cycle around the AGM. The faster BC routes are (a) developers specifying a unit for new towers, (b) purpose-built rental owners, who decide alone, and (c) a Flex lease paid from the operating budget (whether a lease needs a vote depends on the bylaws — check with a strata lawyer).

**Purpose-built rental, serviced and corporate apartments**
- One owner decides. Long-term rental landlords cannot recover GST, so the after-tax price is 5% (BC) or 13% (Ontario) higher. Short-stay furnished operators charging GST/HST can recover it.
- They buy amenities to lease units faster and hold rents. The NMHC/Grace Hill 2024 findings in `market-research.md` (more than half of renters interested in a hot tub or sauna) are US data; no Canadian equivalent was found.

**Hotels**
- The industry norm for the FF&E reserve is **4–5% of total revenue** ([HVS, Rushmore 2003](https://www.hvs.com/Content/1110.pdf) [opened; dated]). Management agreements often specify 4%.
- 2025 performance: Vancouver occupancy **78.4%**, ADR **CAD 284.44**, RevPAR **CAD 223.05** (highest among major Canadian markets); Canada ADR CAD 216.10; Toronto 2025 ADR about **CAD 260.77** (unverified: not in the CoStar release) ([CoStar release](https://www.costar.com/pt-pt/produtos/str-benchmark/recursos/comunicados-de-imprensa/canada-hotels-post-record-setting-performance-2025) [snippet; English page returned 403]; [Hospitality Net copy of the CoStar release](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4130542.html) [opened; confirms the Vancouver and Canada figures]; [Castanet / Vancouver Sun](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Metro-Vancouver/595721/Vancouver-hotel-metrics-fizzle-to-end-sizzling-2025) [snippet]).
- Worked example [E]: a 200-room downtown Vancouver hotel earns 223.05 x 365 x 200 = **CAD 16.3M** in room revenue; at about 70% of total revenue that is about **CAD 23M** total, and a 4% reserve is about **CAD 0.93M a year**. A Lodge (CAD 179k) is about 19% of one year's reserve; a Perch is about 11%. Hotels recover GST/HST, so they compare pre-tax prices.
- A sauna session sold at CAD 45 to 6 guests a day for 300 days a year is about CAD 81k a year in gross session revenue [E]. That payback story beats a pure FF&E argument in limited-service and lifestyle hotels.
