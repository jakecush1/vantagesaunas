Vantage Sauna Co.: Canadian market research (Vancouver, Victoria, Toronto)

Compiled 1 Nov 2026 for a scaled-down Canadian launch. Target segments: hotels, business apartments (purpose-built rental towers plus corporate, furnished and serviced apartments) and condos (strata corporations in BC, condominium corporations in Ontario). Target cities: Vancouver (City and Metro Vancouver), Victoria (Capital Regional District) and Toronto (City and GTA).

How to read this file - Access date. Every source was accessed on 1 Nov 2026 unless a row says otherwise. - Confidence levels. High means a primary source or official dataset that we opened and read. Med means a reputable secondary source quoting primary data. Low means a third-party estimate, a marketing page, or a figure seen only in a search-result excerpt because the page itself was blocked (these are marked "snippet only"). - Estimates. Any number marked (est.) is ours, and the reasoning is given next to it. - Datasets we downloaded and analysed ourselves (high confidence): - CMHC 2025 Rental Market Survey Excel tables for the Vancouver, Toronto and Victoria CMAs. - CBRE monthly hotel statistics published by the Ontario Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming (Dec 2025 and Jul 2026 files). - The City of Toronto apartment building registry (RentSafeTO, refreshed 5 Jul 2026). - The Horwath HTL Canada Hotels & Chains Report 2026 (CoStar data). - The Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO) 2025-26 Annual Report. - The consolidated text of the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 (e-Laws Word file, consolidation period from 31 Dec 2025). - BC's Strata Property Act from BC Laws (current to 27 Oct 2026). - The Bank of Canada exchange-rate series. - Currency. All dollar figures are CAD unless marked USD.

Headline findings

  1. Hotels. The supply is small but runs very full and at high rates. - Rooms in the core cities (CoStar via Horwath HTL, Apr 2026): City of Vancouver about 79 hotels with 13,000 rooms, City of Toronto about 139 hotels with 26,900 rooms, Victoria about 72 hotels with 5,600 rooms. - Vancouver had the highest occupancy and rate of any major Canadian market in 2025: 78.4% occupancy at C$284 ADR (CoStar). Downtown alone ran 80.5% at $342. - Downtown Toronto ran 75.3% occupancy at $350 ADR in 2025 (CBRE). - Greater Victoria reached 78.2% occupancy in 2025 (unverified: seen only in a search excerpt), with August at 94.3% (DGV, verified).
  2. The hotel pipeline is the biggest in Canada. Toronto has 71 projects (11,665 rooms) and Vancouver has 45 projects (8,562 rooms) (Lodging Econometrics, Q3 2026). Extended-stay and apartment-style brands lead the national brand pipeline.
  3. Condos are the largest pool of buildings, but the slowest to sell to. - Unit counts. CMHC counts 316,469 condo apartments in Metro Vancouver, 530,838 in the Toronto CMA and 30,768 in the Victoria CMA (Oct 2025). - Building counts. We estimate about 1,200 projects of 100+ units in Metro Vancouver, 1,700 in the Toronto CMA and 60 in Victoria (est., from CMHC units by project size). - Approval in BC. A rooftop sauna is very likely a "significant change in use or appearance". That needs a 3/4 vote of the votes cast at a general meeting, and paying for it from the contingency reserve fund or a special levy needs a further 3/4 vote. - Approval in Ontario. A change costing more than 10% of the annual budget needs owners of 66⅔% of all units to vote yes. Ontario reserve funds also cannot pay for new amenities.
  4. Ontario has a workable shortcut for larger towers. A Lodge (about C$194k) is under 10% of the annual budget of a Toronto condo of roughly 290 units or more (est.). For those buildings it falls into the board-plus-notice route (owners get 30 days to requisition a meeting) instead of the 66⅔% vote.
  5. Purpose-built rental towers are the fastest path to sales. - One decision-maker. A rental tower has a single owner who decides, so there is no owner vote. - A big, countable stock in Toronto. Toronto's registry lists 891 rental buildings of 10+ storeys with 100+ units (710 of them privately owned). Most were built between 1960 and 1984. - New towers are struggling to fill. In the GTHA, two-thirds of rental buildings completed since 2000 were offering incentives in Q4 2025, and 59% of 2025 completions were still unleased at year end. In Metro Vancouver, buildings with 200+ units had 11.5% vacancy (Oct 2025), and the new stock built since mid-2022 had 14.8%. Owners in that position need amenities that set them apart.
  6. Sauna demand is real and priced. - Vancouver has about 20 Finnish-style circuit sauna venues. Drop-ins cost $24-55 and memberships $130-280 a month. - Toronto. Othership charges $58 a class, and Löyly's floating sauna charges $45 per 75 minutes. - Victoria. HAVN floats in the Inner Harbour, and the Oak Bay Beach Hotel has just added a new sauna. - Hotels already charge for it. Fairmont Pacific Rim sells $40 sessions of 45 minutes on its sixth-floor pool deck. - Regional Nordic spas. Groupe Nordik reports 600,000+ guests a year across its three Canadian spa villages.
  7. Sizing. Converted at USD/CAD 1.396 and before any Canadian cost adjustments: - Sourced universe: about 4,770 buildings (about C$930M at a C$196k blended contract). - Suitable after our filter: about 1,140 buildings (est.), worth C$224M in hardware plus about C$12.8M a year in recurring revenue. - Realistic 5-year obtainable for a small company: about 81 buildings, C$15.8M in cumulative hardware, with recurring revenue running at about C$0.9M a year by the end of 2031. That is about 7% of the suitable pool and about 13% of the 627 buildings in the US plan.
  8. Where to start. Toronto holds about 60% of the obtainable pool. However, Vancouver plus Victoria share one province, one strata law and one building code, and have the strongest hotel economics. Launch in Vancouver and Victoria in 2027 and add Toronto in 2028.

2.3 Amenity trends in new towers

  • New towers already market saunas and plunges. Recent Toronto luxury towers advertise thermal amenities. Concord Canada House lists an indoor pool, hot tub, cold plunge, sauna and steam rooms. Nobu Residences Toronto lists a dry sauna, steam room, cold plunge pool and Japanese soaking tub. A Vancouver-area listing (SkyHaven-Ortus) describes a Level 29 "Rooftop Sky Spa" with hot and cold plunge pools and a steam room. Sources: developer and agent pages found in search (Living Realty, Studio Munge, Trovit listing). Confidence: low. These are marketing pages, and we found no market-wide data.
  • New rental towers too. Rental projects compete the same way. Hazelview's Station House (1141 Bloor St W, Toronto, spring 2026) advertises a "Nordic spa". The 2Fifteen rental in Forest Hill has saunas and a 17th-floor rooftop terrace (Toronto Life, snippet only; page returned 403).
  • The new-build condo channel is weak right now.
  • For the first time this century, rental starts exceeded condo starts in the City of Toronto in 2025 (CMHC Housing Supply Report, Mar 2026).
  • New home starts are expected to fall for a fifth year in 2026, with condos falling most and the GTA hit hardest (CAO 2025-26 Annual Report).
  • Vancouver condo development is held back by weak presales (CMHC).
  • Conclusion: selling to developers for new condo buildings is a small channel for 2027-2029. Retrofitting existing buildings is the main condo market.

2.5 How a BC strata approves and pays for a new amenity (Strata Property Act, current to 27 Oct 2026)

Step Rule Section
Approve the change The strata "must not make a significant change in the use or appearance of common property" unless it is approved "by a 3/4 vote" at an annual or special general meeting. EV charging only needs a majority. A rooftop sauna suite is almost certainly significant (est.; the Civil Resolution Tribunal decides this case by case) s. 71
What "3/4 vote" means 3/4 of the votes cast by eligible voters present in person or by proxy, not counting abstentions. It is not 3/4 of all owners s. 1
Pay from the contingency reserve fund (CRF) The CRF is for expenses that "usually occur less often than once a year or that do not usually occur". Spending on anything that is not a depreciation-report repair or EV work needs a 3/4 vote ss. 92, 96
Pay by special levy Needs a 3/4 vote when each lot's share is set by unit entitlement (s. 99/100); any other split needs a unanimous vote. The resolution must state the purpose, total, each lot's share and the due dates s. 108
Borrow (for example, to finance) Needs a 3/4 vote s. 111
Pay from the operating fund Must be in the approved budget or approved by a 3/4 vote. Unbudgeted spending is capped at the lesser of $2,000 and 5% of operating contributions unless bylaws say otherwise ss. 97-98
Minimum CRF contribution Since 1 Nov 2023, at least 10% of the budgeted operating-fund contribution each year Strata Property Regulation s. 6.1(2), B.C. Reg. 7/2023 (high); start date per BCFSA advisory (med)

In practice (est.). - One meeting. A BC sale ends with one general meeting that passes two 3/4 resolutions: approve the change, and fund it from the CRF or a special levy. The two can be on the same agenda. - Cost per unit. A Lodge at C$194k comes to about $970 per unit in a 200-unit strata, or $1,940 in a 100-unit strata, as a one-off levy. - Lease as an alternative. A lease paid from the operating budget needs the cost in the annual budget, so it is usually passed at the AGM.

2.6 How an Ontario condo board approves and pays (Condominium Act, 1998, consolidation from 31 Dec 2025)

Tier Rule Section
Board only, no notice Allowed if it is legally required, needed for safety, or its cost "in any given month" is no more than the greater of $1,000 and 1% of the annual budgeted common expenses s. 97(2)
Board plus notice to owners The notice must describe the change, its cost and how it will be paid. Owners have 30 days to requisition a meeting, which takes owners of 15% of units (s. 46). The change goes ahead unless owners requisition a meeting and then vote it down ss. 97(3), 46(1)
"Substantial" change If the total cost exceeds 10% of the annual budgeted common expenses (or the board chooses to treat it as substantial), it needs "the owners who own at least 66⅔ per cent of the units" to vote in favour at a meeting ss. 97(4)-(6)
Who pays The cost "shall form part of the common expenses", meaning the operating budget, fee increases or a special assessment s. 97(7)
Reserve fund Can be used "solely for the purpose of major repair and replacement", so it cannot fund a new amenity ss. 93(2), 95(1)
Note e-Laws shows the never-proclaimed 2015 amendment to s. 97 (2015, c. 28, Sched. 1, s. 88) as repealed under s. 10.1 of the Legislation Act on 31 Dec 2025, so the original 1998 thresholds above are the ones in force e-Laws consolidation

Worked thresholds for Toronto (est.). Assume a typical unit of ~700 sq ft (new GTA rentals average 720 sq ft per Urbanation) and fees of ~$0.80 per sq ft per month. - Annual budget per unit: about $6,700. - A 300-unit tower: budget about $2.0M, so the 10% line is about $202k. - Unit count at which each model falls under 10% and so avoids the 66⅔% vote: - Perch (C$109k): about 162+ units. - Lodge (C$194k): about 289+ units. - Summit (C$348k): about 517+ units. - Pay-per-use model (needs a lawyer's opinion). If Vantage owned the suite and residents paid per use, the corporation's own cost could fall under the "no notice" tier. The structure is still an addition to the common elements, so get a legal opinion before relying on this. A lease would likely be judged on its total cost under s. 97(6), which counts total cost "regardless of whether part of the cost is incurred before or after the current fiscal year".

2.7 What this means for the condo sales cycle (est.)

  • BC (Vancouver, Victoria): about 4-9 months. A champion on council takes the proposal to council, and owners usually ask for a structural review and an information session. Owners then vote at the AGM (typically held once a year) or at a special meeting. The 3/4-of-votes-cast standard is achievable with good owner engagement.
  • Ontario (Toronto), larger towers on the notice route: about 3-6 months. The steps are a board resolution, a notice to owners, and the 30-day requisition window.
  • Ontario, buildings where the change is "substantial": 6-12+ months, with a high failure rate. Getting 66⅔% of all units to vote yes is hard in towers where 40-57% of units are rented out (CMHC).
  • What to sell into Ontario condos: Perch or Lodge to towers of 290+ units, a lease or pay-per-use structure, and roll-outs through big property-management firms.

They have flat roofs and elevators, and many once had a pool and sauna. - The owners are portfolio landlords. They are REITs, pension funds and private landlords that decide centrally, which suits portfolio roll-outs. They also watch costs closely, so the pitch has to be repositioning and retaining tenants. - New towers are mostly missing from the registry. Only 68 towers built since 2015 appear, so new towers have to be found through the development pipeline.

5. Bottom-up sizing in CAD

5.1 Suitability filter (applies to all segments)

A building counts as "suitable" only if all of these hold (est.): 1. Roof. A flat roof or podium deck with at least about 40 m² clear of mechanical equipment, reachable by elevator and stair. 2. Height and scale. 8-10+ storeys, or a hotel or tower big enough to carry the cost. For condos, 100+ units, so the suite stays near or under 10% of the annual budget. 3. Ownership. For rentals, a private owner (we exclude TCHC and social housing). For hotels, an owner or brand that allows rooftop amenities. For condos, a strata or condo corporation without a major envelope or levy crisis pending. 4. Budget. Upscale-or-better hotels, Class A/B+ rentals, and condos that can fund about C$110-350k.

5.4 Sensitivities (est.)

  • Price. A Canada price 15% above the straight conversion (for certification, a local factory and wages) lifts 5-year hardware to about C$18.2M. A Perch-heavy mix (60% Perch) cuts it to about C$13M.
  • Condo approval. If Ontario legal advice confirms the pay-per-use route avoids an owner vote, Toronto condos could double to about 30 buildings.
  • Toronto timing. If Toronto entry slips to 2029, the 5-year total falls to about 60 buildings.

What this means for the plan

  1. Re-base the company on about 80 buildings in 5 years, not 627. That is C$16M of hardware and about C$0.9M of recurring run-rate. The US opex plan (US$13.7M in Y5) cannot be carried by these numbers, so the Canadian plan needs a lean team built around one install crew per region.
  2. Launch in Vancouver and Victoria in 2027. They share one province, one strata law (3/4 of votes cast), one building code and one approval culture. Hotels there run at 78-80% occupancy and C$284-342 ADR, and Victoria hotels are already adding saunas (Oak Bay Beach Hotel). Add Toronto in 2028 as the volume market (about 60% of the obtainable pool).
  3. Lead with rental towers and hotels; treat condos as a slower, compounding third channel. - Rental towers. One owner decides. New towers are giving away about two months of free rent, so a sauna that speeds lease-up pays for itself fast. - Hotels. Revenue-share pilots work because Fairmont Pacific Rim already sells $40 Nordic sessions on its sixth-floor (podium) pool deck.
  4. Build two condo playbooks. - BC. A single owner meeting with two 3/4 resolutions: approve the change, and fund it from the contingency reserve fund or a special levy. Bring a ready information-session kit and sample resolutions. - Ontario. Sell Perch or Lodge to towers of 290+ units so the cost stays under 10% of the budget (board plus notice, no 66⅔% vote). Get a legal opinion on a Vantage-owned, pay-per-use model. Never propose paying from the reserve fund, which the Act prohibits.
  5. Sell to the portfolio owners of Toronto's 710 private 1960s-80s rental towers as a repositioning amenity. Pair this with a roof and structure survey product, because these roofs are old.
  6. Price residents' alternative. Local studios charge $35-58 a session and $130-280 a month. Market the building suite as "a $150-a-month membership included in your rent or fees". For hotels, plan on $40-60 per session and $150-170 local day passes.
  7. Make the product Canadian before the first install (design follow-up, not researched here): - Canadian certification marks (cULus / cETLus or CSA) on heaters and the module. - The Canadian Electrical Code, Part I (CSA C22.1:24) rather than the NEC. BC adopts it with BC amendments as the B.C. Electrical Code (Electrical Safety Regulation, B.C. Reg. 100/2004, s. 20), and s. 21 bars using or selling electrical equipment in BC without an accepted certification or approval mark. Ontario adopts it with ESA amendments as the Ontario Electrical Safety Code (O. Reg. 164/99). - BC, Vancouver and Ontario building codes, including snow, wind and seismic loads. Corrected on fact-check (1 Nov 2026): code snow loads are higher in Vancouver than in Toronto, not lower. Ground snow load Ss/Sr is 1.8/0.2 kPa at Vancouver City Hall (BCBC 2018, Appendix C, Table C-2; the BCBC 2024 / Vancouver Building By-law 2025 value is unverified) and 0.9/0.4 kPa at Toronto City Hall (2024 OBC, SB-1, Table 2). Victoria is 1.5/0.3 kPa under BCBC 2024 (City of Victoria bulletin, Mar 2025). The 1-in-50 hourly wind pressure is about the same in Vancouver (0.45 kPa) and Toronto (0.44 kPa), and highest in Victoria (0.57 kPa). Seismic demand is far higher on the coast: Sa(0.2) is 0.848 at Vancouver City Hall and 1.30 in Victoria (BCBC 2018, Table C-3, site class C), against 0.249 for Toronto (SB-1, Table 3, Part 9). BCBC 2024 takes its seismic values from the NBC 2020 Seismic Hazard Tool (Victoria Smax 1.68 for site class C). Restraining the ballasted, zero-penetration module and plunge against earthquakes in BC is therefore a design item for the engineer of record. The exact anchorage rule that applies was not checked here (unverified). - Toronto's Green Roof Bylaw (Municipal Code Ch. 492) for new buildings (City of Toronto).
  8. Use the new-build channel selectively. Condo starts are at multi-year lows, but rental starts (9,821 in the GTHA in 2025) and the hotel pipeline (116 projects in Toronto and Vancouver) are strong. Brief architects of rental and hotel projects in 2027 for 2029-2031 deliveries.
  9. Watch the competition. HAVN wants to come to Vancouver, Therme is planned for Toronto (2029), and Vancouver's roughly 20 sauna studios are fighting for the same customers. Position Vantage as the building's own amenity, not a destination spa.
