1. Brand and company

  • Company: Vantage Sauna Co. (short: Vantage). Working name; trademark search pending.
  • Tagline: The amenity above everything.
  • One-liner: Vantage designs, factory-builds and installs shared rooftop sauna suites for hotels, rental towers and business apartments, and condo buildings, turning unused roof space into the most-loved amenity in the building.
  • What we are: a prefab building-products company with a recurring service and software layer. Not a spa operator.
  • Voice: warm, precise, Nordic-modern. Confident, no hype. Short sentences.
  • Palette: Ember #C8552B (accent), Charcoal #1F1D1B, Birch #F4EFE6, Cedar #8A5A3C, Steam #DCE6E8, Night #0F1A24 (dark mode). Type: "Fraunces" (display) and "Inter" (body), Google Fonts.

  • Company form: Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), incorporated in BC or federally, head office in BC. Keeps the 35% refundable SR&ED credit and small-business tax rate (11% BC, 11.2% ON).

2. Product line (v3 layout + Canadian requirements)

All models: all-electric, factory-built in the Fraser Valley, delivered as an elevator-sized flat-pack panel kit (default for Perch and Lodge; panels no larger than 2.0 x 1.1 m, heaviest piece under 120 kg) or as a craneable module (Summit, or by request where a staging street exists). Hot-room layout: both bench tiers run wall to wall along the back wall and face a full-length, privacy-tinted glass front on the outside wall, so every seat has a view out; the two short side walls are solid wood and carry the door and the heater corner. Smart lock and booking app. Session timers. Seats are bench places at 600 mm; drawn layouts give 5 / 7 / 11 places (7 / 9 / 13 at 550 mm); comfort guideline (3 m3 per person) 4 / 6 / 11.

Canadian requirements built into the product (from 06-research/canada-codes-and-engineering.md): - Base: in Vancouver and Victoria (seismic category SC4) friction may not resist earthquake forces, so the base frame is bolted to the structure with engineered, flashed anchors (4-8 posts) and ballast is minimised. In Toronto a ballasted, non-penetrating base can still pass where the site is SC1/SC2 and wind checks pass. The "zero roof penetrations" claim applies to Toronto only; in BC say "engineered anchors, flashed by a roofer". - Loads: occupied roofs 4.8 kPa (NBC/BCBC/OBC Table 4.1.5.3). Module roof designed for 2.0 kPa snow plus drift (Vancouver 1.64 kPa, Toronto 1.12 kPa). Wind to NBC 4.1.7. - Electrical: heaters carry a Canadian mark (cETLus, cULus or CSA) to CSA C22.2 No. 164-18; 208 V-rated heaters; 600 V buildings get the transformer kit; Canadian Electrical Code; Ontario Rule 62-222: hard-wired 1-hour timer, no override, so the app cannot extend a session. Seek a module listing to C22.2 No. 164 (plus UL 875 for export) and certify the factory to CSA A277. - Accessibility: 850 mm clear door (900 mm leaf), 1,500 mm turning space in the Lodge and Summit hot rooms (CSA B651), barrier-free route to the roof (building's responsibility; Ontario requires it for rooftop amenity spaces). ADA-style transfer section kept as best practice. In BC an indoor shower must be accessible (open item for the Lodge vestibule). - Fire: towers of noncombustible construction need a noncombustible wood-look cladding option (or a CAN/ULC-S134 test), FRT or tested ceiling boards (flame spread 25 or less), sprinkler-ready module with a high-temperature head, horn/strobe tied to the building alarm. - Cold plunge: regulated as a pool in both provinces (BC commercial pool permits; Ontario Class B public cold plunge pool): chlorine or bromine dosing with logging, handrail, clock, signs, emergency stop, health notice.

Construction system (shared spec)

  • Base: hot-dip galvanized steel frame on adjustable feet and rubber load-spreading pads/sleepers that carry the module to beams and columns. Two variants: BC (Vancouver, Victoria) with engineered, flashed structural anchors (4-8 posts) and minimal ballast; Toronto ballasted with parapet tie-back brackets and no penetrations where the site is SC1/SC2 and wind checks pass. Designed for occupied roofs rated 4.8 kPa (NBC/BCBC/OBC).
  • Wall panel (outside -> in): 20 mm thermally modified ash/pine cladding (vertical), ventilated 25 mm cavity, breathable membrane, 12 mm fiber-cement sheathing (non-combustible), 90 mm mineral-wool insulation (non-combustible, Euroclass A1), aluminium-foil vapour barrier rated >= 120 C and <= 0.1 perm, 22 mm ventilated batten gap, 15 mm thermo-aspen interior panelling. Total wall ~185 mm.
  • Roof: standing-seam steel (non-combustible), 3 deg mono-pitch, integrated gutter and downspout to roof drain.
  • Glazing: the whole outside wall of the hot room is a double-glazed tempered insulated glass unit with a privacy-tinted reflective (bronze) outer pane and a low-iron inner pane, thermally broken anodized aluminium frame. By day bathers see out and the roof sees a dark pane; at night the effect reverses, so the night lighting scene keeps the hot room dim (interior blind optional). Side walls are solid wood; the sauna door is a glass leaf in the near side wall.
  • Heater: commercial electric, 208 V 3-phase, Canadian mark (cETLus, cULus or CSA) to CSA C22.2 No. 164-18; remote controller with schedule, hard-wired 1-hour timed cut-off with no override (Ontario Rule 62-222; used everywhere), over-temperature cut-off, door sensor. Vantage seeks a module listing to C22.2 No. 164 (plus UL 875 for export). Wiring to the Canadian Electrical Code (CSA C22.1; B.C. Electrical Code; Ontario ESC). 600 V buildings get the transformer kit. Interior temperature cap 90 C.
  • Benches: thermo-aspen, two tiers along the back wall, side wall to side wall, facing the glass; upper bench 1,150 mm from floor and 700 mm deep (ceiling 1,150-1,250 mm above it, the "law of loyly"); lower bench 700 mm high and 500 mm deep; 350 mm step by the door; the far end of the lower tier is an ADA-style transfer section (best practice; no Canadian sauna clause) 465 mm high, 1,070 mm long, 560 mm deep, with a lift-out lower-bench section beside it that clears the 760 x 1,220 mm transfer space.
  • Door and access: 850 mm clear (900 mm leaf), outward-swinging glass door in the near side wall with a manifestation strip; 1,500 mm turning space inside the hot room (Lodge, Summit; CSA B651) or on the level landing (Perch). Open item: 1,700 mm spaces where BC/OBC treat a changing room as universal.
  • Floor: aluminium grating sub-floor with non-slip composite decking, floor drain to roof drain.
  • Ventilation: mechanical, 9-12 L/s per person; supply above heater, extract low behind benches.
  • Lighting: IP65 LED strips under benches and behind backrests; 2700 K; dimmable scenes.
  • Access and safety: Bluetooth/NFC smart lock tied to Vantage Access, occupancy sensor, emergency stop, 90 C cap, exterior safety signage, first-aid and drinking-water station.
  • Install time on site: Perch 1-2 days, Lodge 2-3 days, Summit 4-6 days. Lead time from signed contract to first session: 12-20 weeks (Perch/Lodge), 16-26 weeks (Summit).

3. Recurring revenue

  • Vantage Care service plan: Perch C$450/mo, Lodge C$700/mo, Summit C$1,150/mo. Quarterly inspections, heater stones, bench refinishing, 24-hour support, parts, plunge water-quality logging. Attach rate 85%.
  • Vantage Access software: C$300/mo per building. Booking, smart-lock access, session timers (within the 1-hour hard cut-off), usage analytics, hotel session pricing. Attach rate 90%. White-label platform at launch.
  • Blended recurring per installed building: C$10,048 per year at 63% gross margin.

4. Commercial models

  1. Purchase (primary): 50% deposit at contract, 40% at delivery, 10% at commissioning. Care + Access monthly. Hotels and short-stay operators recover GST/HST; condos and long-term rental landlords generally cannot, so quote them with tax shown. Get a written BC PST ruling before quoting in BC.
  2. Vantage Flex lease through a Canadian equipment-finance partner (Vantage paid in full at install): from C$2,150/mo (Perch), C$3,850/mo (Lodge), C$6,800/mo (Summit), 84 months, Care + Access included.
  3. Hotel revenue-share pilot (up to 3 lighthouse hotels): C$0 upfront, Vantage takes 35% of session revenue, 5-year term, C$2,000/mo minimum. Vancouver hotels already sell C$40 sessions (Fairmont Pacific Rim).

7. Market (sourced; details and links in 06-research/canada-market-research.md)

  • Hotels: City of Vancouver ~79 hotels / 13,000 rooms (78.4% occupancy, C$284 ADR in 2025; downtown 80.5% at C$342); City of Toronto ~139 hotels / 26,900 rooms (downtown 75.3% at C$350); Victoria ~72 hotels / 5,600 rooms (79.9% occupancy Jan-Aug 2025). Pipeline: Toronto 71 projects / 11,665 rooms; Vancouver 45 / 8,562 (Lodging Econometrics Q3 2026).
  • Condos: CMHC counts 316,469 condo apartments in Metro Vancouver, 530,838 in the Toronto CMA, 30,768 in Victoria; ~1,200 / 1,700 / 60 projects of 100+ units (estimate). BC: a significant change needs a 3/4 vote of votes cast; a special levy needs 3/4; reserve-fund spending needs 3/4, or a majority if the depreciation report recommends it. Ontario: owners of 66 2/3% of all units must vote yes if the cost exceeds 10% of the budget (board-plus-notice below that), and the reserve fund cannot pay for new amenities.
  • Rental towers: Toronto registry lists 891 rental buildings of 10+ storeys and 100+ units (710 private); new towers are struggling to lease (59% of 2025 GTHA completions unleased at year end; 11.5-14.8% vacancy in new Metro Vancouver towers).
  • Demand: ~20 circuit-sauna venues in Vancouver (C$24-55 drop-in, C$130-280/month); Othership Toronto C$58 a class; Fairmont Pacific Rim sells C$40 sessions; HAVN floating sauna in Victoria.
  • Sizing: sourced universe ~4,770 buildings (~C$866M at C$181.5k); suitable ~1,140 buildings (~C$207M hardware at C$181.5k plus ~C$11.5M a year recurring); base plan 122 buildings by 2031 (~11%); research's conservative obtainable 81.
  • Competition: no Canadian firm sells a turnkey rooftop suite; nearest are residential kits and barrels (C$8-15k) and delivered-only commercial cabins (Fritz Tiny Homes from ~C$78k). Hotels' real alternative is the social-sauna boom (Othership, Tevah, HAVN).

8. Go-to-market

Segments in priority order: (1) purpose-built rental towers and business/serviced apartments (one decision-maker; lease-up pressure), (2) upscale and lifestyle hotels (revenue-share pilots), (3) condos and strata (slower; BC and Ontario playbooks with information-session kits and sample resolutions), (4) developers of new rental and hotel projects (brief architects in 2027 for 2029-2031 deliveries). Channels: direct sales to owners and asset managers, property-management firms (strata managers), architects and developers, a demo Lodge in the Fraser Valley and a lighthouse rooftop in Vancouver. Sales cycle: 2-4 months rentals and hotels, 4-9 months condos.
