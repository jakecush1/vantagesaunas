# Vantage Sauna Co.: Canadian market research (Vancouver, Victoria, Toronto)

Compiled 1 Nov 2026 for a scaled-down Canadian launch. Target segments: hotels, business apartments (purpose-built rental towers plus corporate, furnished and serviced apartments) and condos (strata corporations in BC, condominium corporations in Ontario). Target cities: Vancouver (City and Metro Vancouver), Victoria (Capital Regional District) and Toronto (City and GTA).

**How to read this file**
- **Access date.** Every source was accessed on **1 Nov 2026** unless a row says otherwise.
- **Confidence levels.** *High* means a primary source or official dataset that we opened and read. *Med* means a reputable secondary source quoting primary data. *Low* means a third-party estimate, a marketing page, or a figure seen only in a search-result excerpt because the page itself was blocked (these are marked "snippet only").
- **Estimates.** Any number marked **(est.)** is ours, and the reasoning is given next to it.
- **Datasets we downloaded and analysed ourselves** (high confidence):
  - CMHC 2025 Rental Market Survey Excel tables for the Vancouver, Toronto and Victoria CMAs.
  - CBRE monthly hotel statistics published by the Ontario Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Gaming (Dec 2025 and Jul 2026 files).
  - The City of Toronto apartment building registry (RentSafeTO, refreshed 5 Jul 2026).
  - The Horwath HTL *Canada Hotels & Chains Report 2026* (CoStar data).
  - The Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO) 2025-26 Annual Report.
  - The consolidated text of the Ontario *Condominium Act, 1998* (e-Laws Word file, consolidation period from 31 Dec 2025).
  - BC's *Strata Property Act* from BC Laws (current to 27 Oct 2026).
  - The Bank of Canada exchange-rate series.
- **Currency.** All dollar figures are CAD unless marked USD.

## Headline findings

1. **Hotels.** The supply is small but runs very full and at high rates.
   - **Rooms in the core cities** (CoStar via Horwath HTL, Apr 2026): City of Vancouver about 79 hotels with 13,000 rooms, City of Toronto about 139 hotels with 26,900 rooms, Victoria about 72 hotels with 5,600 rooms.
   - **Vancouver** had the highest occupancy and rate of any major Canadian market in 2025: 78.4% occupancy at C$284 ADR (CoStar). Downtown alone ran 80.5% at $342.
   - **Downtown Toronto** ran 75.3% occupancy at $350 ADR in 2025 (CBRE).
   - **Greater Victoria** reached 78.2% occupancy in 2025, with August at 94.3%.
2. **The hotel pipeline is the biggest in Canada.** Toronto has 71 projects (11,665 rooms) and Vancouver has 45 projects (8,562 rooms) (Lodging Econometrics, Q3 2026). Extended-stay and apartment-style brands lead the national brand pipeline.
3. **Condos are the largest pool of buildings, but the slowest to sell to.**
   - **Unit counts.** CMHC counts 316,469 condo apartments in Metro Vancouver, 530,838 in the Toronto CMA and 30,768 in the Victoria CMA (Oct 2025).
   - **Building counts.** We estimate about 1,200 projects of 100+ units in Metro Vancouver, 1,700 in the Toronto CMA and 60 in Victoria (est., from CMHC units by project size).
   - **Approval in BC.** A rooftop sauna is very likely a "significant change in use or appearance". That needs a **3/4 vote of the votes cast** at a general meeting, and paying for it from the contingency reserve fund or a special levy needs a further 3/4 vote.
   - **Approval in Ontario.** A change costing more than 10% of the annual budget needs **owners of 66⅔% of all units** to vote yes. Ontario reserve funds also **cannot** pay for new amenities.
4. **Ontario has a workable shortcut for larger towers.** A Lodge (about C$194k) is under 10% of the annual budget of a Toronto condo of roughly 290 units or more (est.). For those buildings it falls into the board-plus-notice route (owners get 30 days to requisition a meeting) instead of the 66⅔% vote.
5. **Purpose-built rental towers are the fastest path to sales.**
   - **One decision-maker.** A rental tower has a single owner who decides, so there is no owner vote.
   - **A big, countable stock in Toronto.** Toronto's registry lists **891 rental buildings of 10+ storeys with 100+ units** (710 of them privately owned). Most were built between 1960 and 1984.
   - **New towers are struggling to fill.** In the GTHA, two-thirds of rental buildings completed since 2000 were offering incentives in Q4 2025, and 59% of 2025 completions were still unleased at year end. In Metro Vancouver, buildings with 200+ units had 11.5% vacancy (Oct 2025), and the new stock built since mid-2022 had 14.8%. Owners in that position need amenities that set them apart.
6. **Sauna demand is real and priced.**
   - **Vancouver** has about 20 Finnish-style circuit sauna venues. Drop-ins cost $24-55 and memberships $130-280 a month.
   - **Toronto.** Othership charges $58 a class, and Löyly's floating sauna charges $45 per 75 minutes.
   - **Victoria.** HAVN floats in the Inner Harbour, and the Oak Bay Beach Hotel has just added a new sauna.
   - **Hotels already charge for it.** Fairmont Pacific Rim sells $40 sessions of 45 minutes on its sixth-floor pool deck.
   - **Regional Nordic spas.** Groupe Nordik reports 600,000+ guests a year across its three Canadian spa villages.
7. **Sizing.** Converted at USD/CAD 1.396 and before any Canadian cost adjustments:
   - **Sourced universe:** about **4,770 buildings** (about C$930M at a C$196k blended contract).
   - **Suitable after our filter:** about **1,140 buildings** (est.), worth **C$224M** in hardware plus about **C$12.8M a year** in recurring revenue.
   - **Realistic 5-year obtainable** for a small company: about **81 buildings, C$15.8M in cumulative hardware**, with recurring revenue running at about **C$0.9M a year** by the end of 2031. That is about 7% of the suitable pool and about 13% of the 627 buildings in the US plan.
8. **Where to start.** Toronto holds about 60% of the obtainable pool. However, Vancouver plus Victoria share one province, one strata law and one building code, and have the strongest hotel economics. **Launch in Vancouver and Victoria in 2027 and add Toronto in 2028.**

- **The new-build condo channel is weak right now.**
  - For the first time this century, rental starts exceeded condo starts in the City of Toronto in 2025 ([CMHC Housing Supply Report, Mar 2026](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/media-newsroom/news-releases/2026/spring-2026-housing-supply-report)).

  - Vancouver condo development is held back by weak presales (CMHC).
  - **Conclusion:** selling to developers for new condo buildings is a small channel for 2027-2029. Retrofitting existing buildings is the main condo market.

### 2.5 How a BC strata approves and pays for a new amenity (*Strata Property Act*, current to 27 Oct 2026)

| Step | Rule | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Approve the change | The strata "must not make a **significant change in the use or appearance of common property**" unless it is approved "by a **3/4 vote**" at an annual or special general meeting. EV charging only needs a majority. A rooftop sauna suite is almost certainly significant (est.; the Civil Resolution Tribunal decides this case by case) | [s. 71](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_05) |
| What "3/4 vote" means | **3/4 of the votes cast** by eligible voters present in person or by proxy, not counting abstentions. It is **not** 3/4 of all owners | [s. 1](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_01) |
| Pay from the contingency reserve fund (CRF) | The CRF is for expenses that "usually occur less often than once a year or that do not usually occur". Spending on anything that is not a depreciation-report repair or EV work needs a **3/4 vote** | [ss. 92, 96](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_06) |
| Pay by special levy | Needs a **3/4 vote**; the resolution must state the purpose, total, each lot's share and the due dates | [s. 108](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_06) |
| Borrow (for example, to finance) | Needs a **3/4 vote** | [s. 111](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_06) |
| Pay from the operating fund | Must be in the approved budget or approved by a 3/4 vote. Unbudgeted spending is capped at the lesser of $2,000 and 5% of operating contributions unless bylaws say otherwise | [ss. 97-98](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_06) |
| Minimum CRF contribution | Since 1 Nov 2023, at least 10% of the operating fund each year | [BCFSA advisory](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/advisory/strata-property-regulation-changes-form-b-and-contingency-reserve-fund-contributions) (search result, med) |

**In practice (est.).**
- **One meeting.** A BC sale ends with **one general meeting** that passes two 3/4 resolutions: approve the change, and fund it from the CRF or a special levy. The two can be on the same agenda.
- **Cost per unit.** A Lodge at C$194k comes to about **$970 per unit in a 200-unit strata**, or **$1,940 in a 100-unit strata**, as a one-off levy.
- **Lease as an alternative.** A lease paid from the operating budget needs the cost in the annual budget, so it is usually passed at the AGM.

### 2.6 How an Ontario condo board approves and pays (*Condominium Act, 1998*, consolidation from 31 Dec 2025)

| Tier | Rule | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Board only, no notice | Allowed if it is legally required, needed for safety, **or** its cost "in any given month" is no more than **the greater of $1,000 and 1% of the annual budgeted common expenses** | [s. 97(2)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98c19) |
| Board plus notice to owners | The notice must describe the change, its cost and how it will be paid. Owners have **30 days to requisition a meeting**, which takes owners of **15% of units** (s. 46). The change goes ahead unless owners requisition a meeting and then vote it down | [ss. 97(3), 46(1)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98c19) |
| "Substantial" change | If the **total** cost exceeds **10% of the annual budgeted common expenses** (or the board chooses to treat it as substantial), it needs "the owners who own at least **66⅔ per cent of the units**" to vote in favour at a meeting | [ss. 97(4)-(6)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98c19) |
| Who pays | The cost "shall form part of the common expenses", meaning the operating budget, fee increases or a special assessment | [s. 97(7)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98c19) |
| Reserve fund | Can be used "**solely** for the purpose of major repair and replacement", so it **cannot fund a new amenity** | [ss. 93(2), 95(1)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98c19) |
| Note | e-Laws shows the never-proclaimed 2015 amendment to s. 97 (2015, c. 28, Sched. 1, s. 88) as repealed under s. 10.1 of the *Legislation Act* on 31 Dec 2025, so the original 1998 thresholds above are the ones in force | e-Laws consolidation |

**Worked thresholds for Toronto (est.).** Assume a typical unit of ~700 sq ft (new GTA rentals average 720 sq ft per [Urbanation](https://www.urbanation.ca/news/nearly-10000-gtha-rentals-started-contruction-2025)) and fees of ~$0.80 per sq ft per month.
- **Annual budget per unit:** about **$6,700**.
- **A 300-unit tower:** budget about $2.0M, so the 10% line is about $202k.
- **Unit count at which each model falls under 10%** and so avoids the 66⅔% vote:
  - **Perch** (C$109k): about **162+ units**.
  - **Lodge** (C$194k): about **289+ units**.
  - **Summit** (C$348k): about **517+ units**.
- **Pay-per-use model (needs a lawyer's opinion).** If Vantage owned the suite and residents paid per use, the corporation's own cost could fall under the "no notice" tier. The structure is still an addition to the common elements, so get a legal opinion before relying on this. A lease would likely be judged on its total cost under s. 97(6), which counts total cost "regardless of whether part of the cost is incurred before or after the current fiscal year".

### 2.7 What this means for the condo sales cycle (est.)

- **BC (Vancouver, Victoria): about 4-9 months.** A champion on council takes the proposal to council, and owners usually ask for a structural review and an information session. Owners then vote at the AGM (typically held once a year) or at a special meeting. The 3/4-of-votes-cast standard is achievable with good owner engagement.
- **Ontario (Toronto), larger towers on the notice route: about 3-6 months.** The steps are a board resolution, a notice to owners, and the 30-day requisition window.
- **Ontario, buildings where the change is "substantial": 6-12+ months, with a high failure rate.** Getting 66⅔% of **all** units to vote yes is hard in towers where 40-57% of units are rented out (CMHC).
- **What to sell into Ontario condos:** Perch or Lodge to towers of 290+ units, a lease or pay-per-use structure, and roll-outs through big property-management firms.

## 5. Bottom-up sizing in CAD

### 5.1 Suitability filter (applies to all segments)

A building counts as "suitable" only if all of these hold (est.):
1. **Roof.** A flat roof or podium deck with at least about 40 m² clear of mechanical equipment, reachable by elevator and stair.
2. **Height and scale.** 8-10+ storeys, or a hotel or tower big enough to carry the cost. For condos, 100+ units, so the suite stays near or under 10% of the annual budget.
3. **Ownership.** For rentals, a private owner (we exclude TCHC and social housing). For hotels, an owner or brand that allows rooftop amenities. For condos, a strata or condo corporation without a major envelope or levy crisis pending.
4. **Budget.** Upscale-or-better hotels, Class A/B+ rentals, and condos that can fund about C$110-350k.

## What this means for the plan

1. **Re-base the company on about 80 buildings in 5 years, not 627.** That is C$16M of hardware and about C$0.9M of recurring run-rate. The US opex plan (US$13.7M in Y5) cannot be carried by these numbers, so the Canadian plan needs a lean team built around one install crew per region.
2. **Launch in Vancouver and Victoria in 2027.** They share one province, one strata law (3/4 of votes cast), one building code and one approval culture. Hotels there run at 78-80% occupancy and C$284-342 ADR, and Victoria hotels are already adding saunas (Oak Bay Beach Hotel). **Add Toronto in 2028** as the volume market (about 60% of the obtainable pool).
3. **Lead with rental towers and hotels; treat condos as a slower, compounding third channel.**
   - **Rental towers.** One owner decides. New towers are giving away about two months of free rent, so a sauna that speeds lease-up pays for itself fast.
   - **Hotels.** Revenue-share pilots work because Fairmont Pacific Rim already sells $40 rooftop Nordic sessions.
4. **Build two condo playbooks.**
   - **BC.** A single owner meeting with two 3/4 resolutions: approve the change, and fund it from the contingency reserve fund or a special levy. Bring a ready information-session kit and sample resolutions.
   - **Ontario.** Sell Perch or Lodge to towers of 290+ units so the cost stays under 10% of the budget (board plus notice, no 66⅔% vote). Get a legal opinion on a Vantage-owned, pay-per-use model. Never propose paying from the reserve fund, which the Act prohibits.
5. **Sell to the portfolio owners of Toronto's 710 private 1960s-80s rental towers** as a repositioning amenity. Pair this with a roof and structure survey product, because these roofs are old.
6. **Price residents' alternative.** Local studios charge $35-58 a session and $130-280 a month. Market the building suite as "a $150-a-month membership included in your rent or fees". For hotels, plan on $40-60 per session and $150-170 local day passes.
7. **Make the product Canadian before the first install** (design follow-up, not researched here):
   - Canadian certification marks (cULus / cETLus or CSA) on heaters and the module.
   - The Canadian Electrical Code rather than the NEC.
   - BC, Vancouver and Ontario building codes, including snow and wind loads, which are heavier in Toronto than in Vancouver.
   - Toronto's Green Roof Bylaw (Municipal Code Ch. 492) for new buildings ([City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/official-plan-guidelines/green-roof-bylaw/)).
8. **Use the new-build channel selectively.** Condo starts are at multi-year lows, but rental starts (9,821 in the GTHA in 2025) and the hotel pipeline (116 projects in Toronto and Vancouver) are strong. Brief architects of rental and hotel projects in 2027 for 2029-2031 deliveries.
9. **Watch the competition.** HAVN wants to come to Vancouver, Therme is planned for Toronto (2029), and Vancouver's roughly 20 sauna studios are fighting for the same customers. Position Vantage as the building's own amenity, not a destination spa.

## Gaps and estimates

- **Hotel class split by city (estimate).** The 35% upscale-or-better share for the Vancouver and Toronto cores, 25% for Victoria and the 50% roof/owner pass rate are our assumptions. Confirm with a CoStar/STR census by city or the CBRE Hotels Canada market reports.
- **Metro Vancouver hotel rooms.** The 23,000 figure comes from a 2023 council report seen only as a search excerpt. The City of Vancouver pipeline (22 projects / 4,200 rooms) is from Q1 2025.
- **Victoria 2025 ADR.** The ~$280 is estimated from percentage changes. DGV's Feb 2026 year-end column (78.2% occupancy) was read only as an excerpt.
- **Condo project counts (estimate).** These are derived from CMHC units divided by assumed average project sizes (170 / 150-650 / 130). They are not building counts, and one project can contain several towers. The CAO publishes no Toronto split by building size, and BC's LTSA publishes no regional strata split.
- **Rental tower counts for Vancouver (~190) and Victoria (~25), and for the GTA outside the City of Toronto (~145), are estimates.** Only the City of Toronto has a building-level registry. CMHC's portal can produce structure-size tables that we could not extract.
- **New towers in 2027-2031** (Toronto ~120, Vancouver ~80, Victoria ~15) are estimates from pipeline and approvals data.
- **Condo fees.** Toronto ~$0.81 per sq ft comes from 2024 condos.ca data. Vancouver and BC figures are realtor guides. The worked 10% thresholds assume 700 sq ft units at $0.80 per sq ft.
- **Legal points needing counsel:**
  - Whether a rooftop sauna is a "significant change" under BC s. 71.
  - How an Ontario lease or pay-per-use structure is costed under s. 97(6).
  - Any condo declarations or bylaws that restrict roof use.
- **Prices.** Othership's $58 is from a Mar 2026 review, not Othership's own price page. HAVN's price is the founder's estimate before opening. Thermëa and Scandinave prices come from a secondary guide.
- **No Canadian data on spa revenue per room, or on rent or condo-price premiums for saunas.** Use the US proxies and measure at the pilots.
- **Pricing in CAD is a straight currency conversion of US prices.** Canadian certification, freight, labour, any cross-border tariffs, and harmonized and provincial sales taxes are not included.
- **Obtainable shares** (hotels 12-20%, rentals 12-18%, condos 3.7-7%) and the year-by-year phasing are planning assumptions, not data.
