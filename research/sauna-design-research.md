### Sizing rule
- **Base rule: about 1 kW per m3 of hot-room volume.** Harvia's electric-heater catalogue adds that **each square metre of uninsulated stone, glass or similar surface raises the requirement as much as adding 1.2 m3 to the room**, and that heat-storing surfaces (brick, concrete) push it up further. Source: [Harvia electric sauna heaters catalogue on ArchiExpo](https://pdf.archiexpo.com/pdf/harvia/harvia-electric-sauna-heaters/9998-15185.html) [search excerpt only]. Harvia's own sizing article refers users to its calculator and says outdoor or poorly insulated saunas and cold climates need "a more powerful heater" ([harvia.com, How do you choose the right heater](https://harvia.com/en/ideas-and-trends/products/how-do-you-choose-the-right-wood-burning-heater) [page opened]).
- A non-Harvia manual uses 1.5 m3 per m2 of uninsulated brick, tile or glass ([Dancover manual](https://m.dancovershop.com/product-images/pdf/he192010.pdf) [search excerpt only]). US retailers use +15% volume per glass surface, or +12% (uninsulated wall) / +21% (glass) ([Haven of Heat sizing chart](https://havenofheat.com/zh/blogs/sauna-heater-guides/sauna-heater-sizing-chart-calculator-electric-infrared-wood) [search excerpt only]).
- Thermory's dealer FAQ: with a wide back window, "size up your heater (e.g., choose a 9kW over a 6kW)" ([Sauna Marketplace Thermory FAQ](https://saunamarketplace.com/?p=274597) [search excerpt only]).
- Manufacturer volume ratings above are the binding check: pick a heater whose rated range contains the **effective** volume (actual m3 + 1.2 m3 per m2 of glass).

## 4. Design best practice

Primary sources opened: Trumpkin's Notes on Building a Sauna ([localmile.org](https://www.localmile.org/trumpkins-notes-on-building-a-sauna/) [page opened, both halves]) and Finnleo's construction guide ([finnleo.com/sauna-construction](https://finnleo.com/sauna-construction) [page opened]). Retailer "Rule of 230" pages are listed as secondary ([Haven of Heat](https://havenofheat.com/blogs/sauna-guides/sauna-ceiling-height-bench-height-the-finnish-rule-of-230) [search excerpt only]). No Finnish Sauna Society page was retrievable; Trumpkin cites the Finnish RT card for the 3 m3/person figure.

Ceiling height and the law of löyly
- "The first law of löyly" (Pälsi's law): feet above the stones and above the cold zone (lower third of the room volume). (Trumpkin)
- Good minimum hot room: "About 250x250x260cm (8' x 8' x 8.4' high)". Never below 180 x 160 cm plan. (Trumpkin)
- Sitting bench 110-120 cm below the ceiling, "Ideally 120cm (48")". For taller ceilings raise benches 2 cm per 3 cm of extra height. (Trumpkin)
- Finnleo: average sauna ceiling 84 in (213 cm), minimum 77 in, maximum 96 in. (Finnleo)
- Retail "Rule of 230": bench 110 cm + 120 cm headroom = 230 cm ceiling. (Haven of Heat, secondary)

Benches
- Foot bench at least 85 cm (34 in) above the floor and ~10 cm above the stones; sitting bench 40-45 cm above the foot bench (as little as 25 cm). (Trumpkin)
- Sitting-bench depth 24-28 in (61-71 cm) minimum; up to 40 in (100 cm) for the top bench; at least one lay-down bench 76-80 in (193-203 cm). Width per person 60 cm (Europe), 26-30 in suggested for US users. (Trumpkin)
- Volume per person: 3 m3 minimum (Finnish RT card), 4-6 m3 for a better sauna. (Trumpkin)
- Finnleo: "Factor in about 2' of upper bench space per bather." (Finnleo)
- Benches at least 1/6 air-permeable; slat gap ~15 mm (10 mm minimum); 3-5 cm gap between bench and wall; skirts at least 30% open; slats roughly 1x4 nominal. (Trumpkin)
- Clear space heater to foot bench at least 90 cm; ideally ~8 ft heater wall to bench wall. (Trumpkin)
- ADA bench (where the sauna must be accessible): 42 in long minimum, 20-24 in deep, seat 17-19 in above floor, back support 42 in long from <= 2 in to >= 18 in above the seat, 250 lb load, slip-resistant in wet locations. ([US Access Board guide, Saunas and Steam Rooms](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-6-saunas/) [page opened])

Ventilation
- Airflow 9-12 L/s (20-25 CFM) per person; target CO2 below 700 ppm. (Trumpkin)
- Supply: in the ceiling above the heater for downdraft ventilation; exhaust: mechanical, on the back wall below the foot platform; "mechanical downdraft for electric heaters". (Trumpkin)
- Finnleo (passive scheme): intake "directly under the heater", exhaust "opposite your heater in-vent", ideally 2 ft above the floor. (Finnleo)
- Thermostat ~1 m above the sitting bench, 20-30 cm from the corner, no closer than 7 cm to the ceiling; Trumpkin notes UL's position (over the heater, 4-6 in below the ceiling, 90 C limit) "causes problems". (Trumpkin)

Insulation and foil vapour barrier
- Foil is the only vapour barrier; kraft-backed foil rated for sauna temperatures, perm rating 0.1 or less, rated at least 120 C; no bubble-wrap foil; foil-faced PIR preferred. (Trumpkin)
- Air gap 10-20 mm between foil and interior boards on furring; one furring layer always vertical (vertical cladding needs two layers); ceiling boards stop 1 in short of the wall foil; wall cladding stops at least 1 in above the floor. (Trumpkin)
- Mineral wool in the exterior wall 1-2 in minimum (Trumpkin); Finnleo: walls minimum R12, ceiling minimum R16. (Finnleo)

Interior woods
- Soft wood on at least 90% of wall area (at least 65% of each wall); spruce, fir, pine and alder for walls; abachi, alder, aspen and linden for benches; "Avoid cedar" and redwood. (Trumpkin)
- Finnleo standard is Nordic white spruce with abachi/hemlock/cedar benches; Harvia Kuusi uses linden; Iglucraft and Kirami use aspen / heat-treated aspen (see product table). The brief's thermo-aspen interior is consistent with Kirami's commercial-grade Finnish product.

Door and glass
- Door opens outward with no latch; ~22 in width works for a 6 ft 4 in door, 5 ft 6 in or less preferred for heat retention; glass tempered in a wood frame; "Glass should be limited." (Trumpkin)
- Finnleo: "An all-glass door has become the standard." (Finnleo)
- ADA: 32 in minimum clear width, 5 lbf opening force, threshold <= 1/2 in, viewing panel bottom edge <= 43 in. (Access Board)

Floor and drainage
- Linear drain under the benches with two sloped faces; keep the trap wet; heated concrete/tile best, sloped wood T&G acceptable. (Trumpkin)
- Finnleo NorthStar uses an "integral waterproof vinyl surface" floor. (Finnleo product page)
- ADA floors: stable, firm, slip resistant (302). (Access Board)

Hygiene cycle
- After use hold at least 65 C (149 F) at the foot and sitting benches for 15-20 minutes. (Trumpkin)

Weight per m2 of a timber sauna cabin
- No manufacturer publishes kg/m2. From the product table: insulated prefab cabins run **~200-240 kg/m2 of footprint dry** (Finnleo NorthStar 220, Kirami FinVision 217, Thermory Square 241); uninsulated log cabins 90-160 kg/m2. The brief's wall build (12 mm fibre cement, 90 mm mineral wool, standing-seam steel roof, galvanized steel base frame, aluminium grating floor, 10 mm glass, 100-130 kg of heater stones) is heavier than any of those products, so Vantage's modules should be expected to exceed 240 kg/m2 before ballast.

## 5. Rooftop engineering and code

Roof live loads (NYC launch market; NYC Building Code 2022 Table 1607.1, which follows IBC)
- Ordinary flat, pitched and curved roofs (not occupiable): **20 psf [0.96 kPa]**.
- Occupiable roofs: roof gardens **100 psf [4.79 kPa]**; assembly areas **100 psf**; "other similar areas" per Note 1.
- Assembly areas with movable seats and lobbies: 100 psf. Balconies and decks: 1.5 x the live load of the occupancy served, not required to exceed 100 psf. Corridors (first floor) 100 psf.
- Roof surfaces subject to maintenance workers: a 300 lb load (the fetched table rendered it as a psf figure; in IBC Table 1607.1 this is listed as a 300 lb concentrated load; verify in the printed table).
- Source: [NYC Building Code 2022, Chapter 16 on UpCodes](https://up.codes/viewer/new_york_city/nyc-building-code-2022/chapter/16/structural-design) [page opened]. General ASCE 7 commentary: roof live load reduction applies only between 12 and 20 psf for ordinary roofs; a roof deck used by people takes the live load of the occupancy served ([Eng-Tips ASCE 7 4.7.3 thread](https://eng-tips.com/threads/asce-7-section-4-7-3.512950) [search excerpt only]).
- Implication: a sauna module and its users must be checked against the roof's **design** live load (100 psf only if the roof was designed as an occupiable deck), with the module's point loads at its feet; many ordinary flat roofs are designed for 20 psf plus snow, which cannot carry a sauna without strengthening.

Wind on rooftop structures (ASCE 7-16, Section 29.5.1; same provisions numbered 29.4.1 in some editions)
- Lateral: Fh = qh (GCr) Af with **GCr = 1.9** for rooftop structures and equipment with Af less than 0.1Bh, reducing linearly to 1.0 as Af increases to Bh.
- Uplift: Fv = qh (GCr) Ar with **GCr = 1.5** for Ar less than 0.1BL, reducing linearly to 1.0.
- ASCE 7-16 removed the earlier 60 ft building-height limit, so the provisions apply to rooftop structures on buildings of all heights.
- Source: [STRUCTURE magazine, Wind Loads on Non-Building Structures](https://www.structuremag.org/article/wind-loads-on-non-building-structures/) [page opened]; corroborated by [ASCE Standards Exchange thread](https://collaborate.asce.org/standards-exchange/question/cylindrical-rooftop-equipment-wind-pressures) [search excerpt only].
- Ballasted non-penetrating supports: ballast resists uplift and sliding with weight on a wide footprint; the required mass is computed from the site's ASCE 7 design wind speed and base geometry; cable bracing can add lateral restraint; no roof penetrations are required ([Roofing Magazine, Wind loading rooftop equipment](https://roofingmagazine.com/wind-loading-rooftop-equipment/2/), [US patent 9,416,544 non-penetrating roof-mounted support](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9416544) [search excerpt only]). The brief's ballast + parapet tie-back approach is consistent with this; the ballast adds to the roof load and must be in the structural check.

UL 875
- Title: **UL 875, Electric Dry-Bath Heaters**, Edition 10, published and ANSI-approved 8 July 2024.
- Scope 1.1: "electric dry-bath heating equipment and other equipment rated 600 volts or less" producing a dry-heat environment, installed under NFPA 70. Three configurations: (a) a heater unit built or assembled in the field in a special room; (b) **a heater unit combined with a prefabricated rigid room**; (c) a heater combined with a rigid cabinet enclosing all but the user's head.
- Scope 1.2 exclusions: steam-bath heaters and cable-type radiant heating.
- Source: [UL Standards store, UL 875](https://www.shopulstandards.com/ProductDetail.aspx?productId=UL875) [page opened]; [ANSI webstore listing](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/ul/ul875ed102024) [search excerpt only].
- Model-code hooks: California Mechanical Code 939.1 "Electric sauna heaters. Sauna heaters shall comply with UL 875" ([gocodebook CMC 939.1](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/ca/ca-mechanical-code/chapter-9-installation-of-specific-appliances/939.1-electric-sauna-heaters.-sauna-heaters-shall-comply-with-ul-875-and-shall-be-installed-in-a)); Seattle Residential Code chapter 19 likewise ([seattle.gov](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/sdci/codes/seattleresidentialcode/2015srcchapter19.pdf)) [search excerpt only].
- Configuration (b) means Vantage can pursue listing of **the heater plus the prefabricated room as a unit**, which is what the brief's UL/ETL budget line should target.

NEC
- The NEC has no sauna-specific Part. Sauna heaters are installed under **Article 424 (Fixed Electric Space-Heating Equipment)** and/or **Article 422 (Appliances)**, plus 110.3(B) (follow listing and installation instructions). Vendor summary: a disconnect that is readily accessible and within sight of the unit, or lockable if out of sight ([Peak Saunas, 2023 NEC sauna requirements](https://peaksaunas.com/blogs/wellness/2023-nec-sauna-requirements) [search excerpt only; 429]). Electricians' forums cite listing and labelling per ANSI/UL 875 as the compliance point and note no GFCI requirement for a sauna heater under 210.8 ([Mike Holt forum, sauna thread](https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/sauna.137125/); [Mike Holt, residential sauna GFCI](https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/residential-sauna-heater-gfci-protected.2563426/post-2701668) [search excerpt only]). The brief's phrase "NEC Article 424 sauna heater requirements" should be reworded to "installed per NEC Articles 422/424 and the heater's listing instructions"; the NEC text itself was not opened in this pass.

Fire and clearances
- Tylö install sheet: minimum wall distance 4 in for 2.2-8.3 kW heaters and 8-16 in for 10.7-16 kW ([Saunafin Tylö install sheet](https://www.saunafin.com/uploads/documents/469ea19affb7874b4f3b9c604a4e7c999cce3814.pdf) [search excerpt only]). Harvia's US product pages do not publish clearances; they are in each model's manual.
- Trumpkin: heaters' high-limit cut-outs are set very high (his "appears to be 350 F"); keep at least 90 cm from heater to foot bench; Iki-type heaters carry "over 1300 lbs of stones" (relevant to weight).
- UL-listed US heaters are capped around 194-195 F (90 C) versus 230 F for CE-only units ([Haven of Heat HIVE review](https://havenofheat.com/blogs/sauna-guides/huum-hive-review) [search excerpt only]); the brief's 100 C cap is not reachable with a UL/ETL-listed heater.
- Non-combustible cladding: the brief's 12 mm fibre-cement sheathing and standing-seam steel roof address exterior fire exposure; Das Weitzer (Graz) uses aluminium slats on its hotel rooftop sauna (see precedents).

ADA (2010 Standards 241 and 612; Access Board guide [page opened])
- Where provided, saunas and steam rooms must be accessible; in a cluster, 5% but at least one of each type (gender, wet/dry) must comply. For a single rooftop sauna, that sauna must comply.
- 612: a 60 in turning circle or T-turn inside the room; one fixed bench per 903 (dimensions above) with a 30 x 48 in clear floor space at its end that doors may not swing into; readily removable benches may sit in the turning space.
- Doors per 404 (32 in clear, 5 lbf, 1/2 in threshold); controls per 309 (reach range, one-hand, 5 lbf); hooks/shelves within reach range; ISA signage recommended.
- The accessible route to the roof (elevator landing to the sauna door, level landings) is the building's responsibility and is a site-selection filter.

Crane lifts in NYC
- NYC DOB Cranes & Derricks: three permit types (Certificate of Operation, On-site Certificate (CN), On-site Certificate Limited). Fees: Certificate of Approval for a boom under 200 ft USD 500 (USD 300 for boom <= 50 ft over 3 tons); CN on-site inspection, boom up to 250 ft USD 150; weekend inspection USD 750; after-hours/weekend variance USD 100 plus USD 80 per day; amendment USD 100. Permits must be posted on the fence; a licensed rigger and DOT street permits are referenced but not detailed on the page. Source: [nyc.gov DOB Cranes & Derricks permit page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/property-or-business-owner/cranes-derricks-permit.page) [page opened]. DOT issues 150+ sidewalk/roadway permit types ([NYC Open Data, Street Construction Permits - Cranes](https://catalog-beta.data.gov/dataset/street-construction-permits-cranes) [search excerpt only]); the DOT fee schedule was not retrieved.
- Rental cost: no NYC-specific published rate was found. Generic sources (low quality, flagged): small mobile cranes USD 200-1,000/day; an illustrative 100-ton crawler with operator USD 3,800/day, USD 6,900 all-in with delivery, rigging and permits; urban street-closure permits adding USD 500-3,000; "USD 250/hour in Manhattan" for a mobile tower crane ([BigRentz NY crane pages](https://www.bigrentz.com/rental-locations/new-york/new-hyde-park/cranes) and aggregator articles [search excerpt only]). Treat as order-of-magnitude only; Vantage should get three NYC rigger quotes for a 4-6 t lift to 60-150 ft including flagging, DOT permits and off-hours premium. The Hotel Indigo LES rooftop pool (two prefabricated sections hoisted 133 ft) shows the approach is routine for prefabricated amenities.

## 7. Design takeaways for Vantage

1. **Size heaters on effective volume, not floor area.** With a full-height glass front the hot rooms are ~16 / 25 / 37 m3 effective. Specify Perch 10.5-12 kW, Lodge 16-18 kW, Summit 20-24 kW (see section 3 table). 9 kW on Perch and 2 x 9 kW on Summit are under-rated for the glass and outdoor exposure, and twin 9 kW units double the controller/wiring cost without reaching the needed rating.
2. **Specify ETL/UL-875-listed commercial heaters only.** Harvia Virta Pro 16 (240 V 1-ph) and Virta Pro 20 (208 V 3-ph) are ETL-listed and marketed for hotels and apartment buildings; Harvia Club is dealer-listed as UL/ETL; Tylö's North American Sense line claims UL 875. HUUM HIVE (CE/SGS) and EOS (CE) are not UL-listed per US dealers, so they do not satisfy the brief's listing requirement despite their commercial reputation.
3. **Rewrite the temperature cap to 90 C (194 F)**, which is where UL/ETL-listed heaters limit; 100 C is not achievable with a listed unit.
4. **Raise interior ceiling to 2.3-2.4 m.** The brief's exterior heights (2.6 / 2.7 / 2.8 m) leave roughly 2.15-2.35 m inside after floor and roof build-up, below the Finnish 2.5-2.6 m ideal and only just inside Finnleo's 77-96 in range. Add 0.25-0.3 m to each exterior height (check against local zoning height limits for rooftop accessory structures).
5. **Bench geometry:** keep the upper bench at 1,050-1,100 mm but make it 650-700 mm deep (24-28 in minimum per Trumpkin; 600 mm is below the minimum). Treat the 600 mm "lower bench" as a foot bench and add a ~350 mm step; aim for 1,100-1,200 mm from sitting bench to ceiling. Provide at least one lay-down bench of 1.95-2.05 m in Lodge and Summit.
6. **Add an ADA bench and turning circle.** One fixed bench 1,070 mm long x 510-610 mm deep x 430-480 mm high with back support, a 760 x 1,220 mm clear space at its end, and a 1,525 mm turning circle inside each hot room; door 815 mm clear, outward swing, no latch, viewing panel bottom <= 1,090 mm. On a single-sauna roof the one sauna must comply.
7. **Restate capacities as bench seats.** At the Finnish 3 m3/person minimum, the hot rooms hold about 3-4 (Perch), 6 (Lodge) and 9 (Summit) bathers comfortably; Finnleo's 2 ft of upper bench per bather gives 3-4, 4-5 and 5-6 on a single upper bench, more with an L-layout. Market "seats" (upper + lower) and keep the headline ranges only if the L-layout bench lengths support them.
8. **Limit and upgrade the glass.** Every m2 of single glass costs ~1.2 m3 of heater capacity and heat. Use double-glazed tempered units (Kirami and Iglucraft both do) and consider a 1.2-1.5 m wide fixed panel plus glass door rather than a full-width front, or keep the full front and accept the larger heater.
9. **Ventilation numbers:** supply in the ceiling above the heater, mechanical extract low on the back wall under the foot bench, 9-12 L/s per person: Perch 55-70 L/s (115-150 CFM), Lodge 90-120 L/s, Summit 145-190 L/s; add a CO2 sensor (target < 700 ppm) tied to the extract fan via Vantage Access. The brief's description already matches the Finnish downdraft scheme.
10. **Foil and gap details:** foil rated >= 120 C, <= 0.1 perm, no bubble foil, foil as the only vapour barrier; the 22 mm batten gap is fine; one batten layer vertical (two layers for vertical boards); ceiling boards stop 25 mm short of the wall foil; wall boards stop 25 mm above the floor.
11. **Woods:** thermo-aspen interior and benches are the right call (Kirami uses heat-treated aspen); avoid cedar inside the hot room even though US products (Plunge, Hewing) use it; spruce or alder are acceptable wall alternatives.
12. **Floor:** keep the drain but detail it as a linear drain under the benches with a trap that Vantage Care keeps primed; confirm the composite decking is slip-resistant wet and "stable, firm" for ADA.
13. **Hygiene cycle in software:** after the last booking, hold 65 C at the benches for 15-20 minutes before shutdown; make this a default in Vantage Access.
14. **Weight and ballast:** plan on >= 300 kg/m2 dry (the brief's assemblies are heavier than the 200-240 kg/m2 insulated products on the market), plus ballast sized per ASCE 7 GCr 1.9 lateral / 1.5 uplift, plus occupants (~90 kg each) and, on Summit, 1.9 t of plunge. Replace the "<= 50 psf dead + live" target with "designed for roofs rated 100 psf occupiable; point loads at feet published per model; structural engineer letter per site".
15. **Panel kit first, crane second (NYC).** Iglucraft and Kirami ship fully assembled, Plunge ships modular for a 2-3 hour assembly. Given DOB crane permits (USD 150-750 inspections plus Certificate of Approval), DOT street permits and off-hours premiums, make the elevator-sized panel kit the default for Perch and Lodge in dense cores and reserve the craneable module for Summit and sites with a staging street.
16. **Permitting path (NYC):** structural engineer's roof-load letter; condo/co-op board or owner approval; DOB alteration to amend the Certificate of Occupancy for the roof's new use (per Habitat); electrical permit under NEC 422/424 and the heater's listing; crane CN/CD if lifted. Build these into the 6-10 week lead time and the USD 8-25k site-prep line.
17. **Pursue UL 875 configuration (b)** - "heater combined with a prefabricated rigid room" - so the whole module, not just the heater, carries the listing mark. This is a stronger sales claim than a heater-only listing.
18. **Cold plunge spec:** keep ~1,200 L (a true multi-user tub) but specify a 1.5-2 HP commercial chiller with ozone/AOP sanitation and a 20 micron filter, benchmarked on Blue Cube C3X / Modular (USD 20.5-29k) rather than consumer 400 L units; 120 V 20 A is enough for a 1 HP chiller, larger chillers will need a 240 V circuit.
19. **Operating benchmarks for the hotel model:** Löyly EUR 19-29 per 2 h session, Sydney rooftop AUD 60 per person, CopenHot DKK 1,200 per hour for a group sauna, Hewing guests-only 7:00-24:00. The brief's USD 35-60 per guest sits inside this range.
20. **Pricing position:** Perch at USD 78k installed is ~2.5x an assembled Kirami FinVision (GBP 22.5k) and ~0.8x an Iglucraft Panoramic (USD 99.9k + freight, two rooms, 17 m2). It is defensible only as a turnkey commercial package (steel base, engineering, crane/lift, electrical, listing, smart access). Lodge at USD 139k and Summit at USD 249k have no direct prefab comparables; the nearest commercial analogues are site-built hotel saunas. Publish a hardware / engineering / install breakdown on the spec sheet so buyers can compare like for like.
